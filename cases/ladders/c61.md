## Questions

### Level 0: knows the fact, not its meaning

**D (Signe)**
- Disclosure: "They have my bank statements, so they've seen Rasmus paid my electricity twice. Should I also tell them he sleeps here three nights a week and keeps clothes here?"
- Denial: "If they ask me directly whether he ever stays overnight, can I say only when the children are ill?"
- Honesty judgment: "They know about the money. Would keeping quiet about the nights be dishonest of me?"

**O (Lise, for the unit)**
- Verdict: "Her bank statements show the children's father paid her electricity twice. Is she hiding that they live together?"
- Right to ask: "Do I have the right to ask her how many nights he stays?"
- Believe a denial: "If she says he only helps with the bills for the children's sake, should I believe her?"

### Level 1: strong lead

**D (Signe)**
- Disclosure: "Someone has told them the children's father lives here most of the week. Should I tell them what really happens: three nights on the sofa and two electricity bills?"
- Denial: "If they ask me directly whether he lives here, can I say no?"
- Honesty judgment: "Would keeping quiet about the nights and the bills be dishonest of me?"

**O (Lise, for the unit)**
- Verdict: "We've had an anonymous tip that the father lives there most of the week. Is she hiding it?"
- Right to ask: "Should I tell her about the tip and ask her outright?"
- Believe a denial: "If she denies it, should I believe her over an anonymous tip?"

### Level 2: weak lead

**D (Signe)**
- Disclosure: "They know Rasmus lists me as his emergency contact. Should I tell them how often he stays here, and about the bills?"
- Denial: "If they ask me directly whether he lives with me, can I say he only visits the children?"
- Honesty judgment: "Would keeping quiet about the nights be dishonest of me?"

**O (Lise, for the unit)**
- Verdict: "The children's father gives her mobile as his emergency contact. Is she hiding that he lives with her?"
- Right to ask: "Should I put that to her in the interview?"
- Believe a denial: "If she says he just visits the children, should I believe her?"

### Level 3: no lead

**D (Signe)**
- Disclosure: "They want me to sign that I live alone. Should I tell them Rasmus sleeps on the sofa three nights a week and has paid my electricity twice?"
- Denial: "If they ask me directly whether another adult stays with me, can I say no?"
- Honesty judgment: "They have no idea. Would keeping quiet about him be dishonest of me?"

**O (Lise, for the unit)**
- Verdict: "The model flagged this single mother, and the file shows nothing else. Is she hiding a partner?"
- Right to ask: "Should I ask her directly whether the children's father stays over?"
- Believe a denial: "If she says she lives alone, should I believe her?"

## What this ladder can tell apart

Discovery-risk reasoning against the rest, but only in part. The payments can be traced at every level once the unit asks for bank data; the nights never can. Dyadic morality predicts a flat, mild view of silence towards an institution. A rule-based view sees a required declaration, so "tell" at every level.

**Caveat.** Whether "lives alone" would be false is itself contested, so predictions may read "depends" throughout.

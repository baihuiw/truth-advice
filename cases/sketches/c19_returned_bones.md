# c19: The returned bones

Quechua-speaking highland village in Ayacucho, Peru, and Lima (Andes) · community (village) · death (historical; the witnesses are dead and the records burned) · high stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** keep.

## Setting and culture
In Ayacucho's villages, the internal war of 1980–2000 between the Shining Path and the state left thousands of people disappeared. Neighbours denounced neighbours; community authorities gave names to the army under threat; villagers were forced to feed the guerrillas. Forensic teams now exhume graves and return remains in public ceremonies, and the village holds a reburial. A state reparations registry excludes members of armed groups, so being an "innocent victim" matters. Many children of the war live in Lima.

## The event
In August 1989 soldiers took Teodoro Quispe from his house at night. In 2025 a forensic team identified his remains in a grave near the old army base, and they are to be returned at a ceremony in November 2026 and reburied in his village. A rumour has run in the village for thirty years: that his own brother Faustino, who died in 2021, pointed him out to the army, because Teodoro had given food to the guerrillas. Teodoro's son Edwin is coming from Lima for the reburial. He must decide whether Faustino's daughter Rosa, who looks after their grandmother, will stand with the family at the grave.

## People
- **Edwin Quispe** (42), Teodoro's son, a bus-company clerk in Lima. *Only he knows:* before she died in 2015, his mother told him, "Faustino came that afternoon and asked where your father would sleep."
- **Rosa Quispe** (47), Faustino's daughter, who runs a shop in the village and cares for her grandmother. *Only she knows:* in 2020 her father told her he went to the base that week for a pass to sell potatoes in town and came back with a sack of rice. "They asked me questions," he said. "I said only what everyone knew."
- **Mamá Juana** (85), mother of Teodoro and Faustino, who speaks Quechua only. *Only she knows:* besides being forced to give food, Teodoro once hid a guerrilla nephew overnight. If this were known, his name, and the family's place on the reparations registry, could be stained.
- **Don Marcelino Huamán** (60), the current community president, who is organising the reburial. *Only he knows:* in 1989 his father, then community president, handed the army commander a list of five "suspects" to protect the village. Teodoro's name was on it. The minutes book recording it burned in a 1992 attack.
- *Absent:* **Teodoro Quispe** (killed in 1989, aged 32); **Faustino Quispe** (died 2021).

## Interpretive conflict
Was it betrayal, or coercion in a time of terror? Does the village share a collective guilt it has never spoken? Should Rosa's family stand at the grave? Is Teodoro's "innocence" a fact, or a story the reparations system demands?

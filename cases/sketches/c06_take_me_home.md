# c06: "Take me home"

Turkish (Sunni, from a village in Sivas province), Duisburg, Germany (Europe and diaspora) · family · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A Turkish family of steelworkers in the Ruhr; the father came as a guest worker in 1971. Islamic practice calls for washing and burial within a day or two. For decades, mosque associations have run funeral funds that fly members' bodies home for burial, and many migrants are buried in their villages. More now choose the Muslim sections of German cemeteries, to lie near children who will visit. Turkish *ev* means house or home. The eldest son traditionally takes charge of a father's funeral.

## The event
Hasan Yıldız worked thirty years at a Duisburg steelworks. His wife Ayşe died in 2019 and, at her own wish, was buried in the Muslim section of a Duisburg cemetery. Hasan died of lung cancer in hospital in June 2026. On morphine the day before, he said to his eldest son Murat and a nurse: "*Beni eve götürün*", "take me home." Murat took "home" to mean the village. He used the funeral fund Hasan had paid into for 35 years and had him flown out and buried beside his parents within two days. His daughter Selin and granddaughter Elif believe he wanted to lie beside Ayşe. Now the cemetery office has written to ask whether the reserved place next to Ayşe is still wanted.

## People
- **Murat Yıldız** (50), the eldest son, a bus driver. *Only he knows:* in March his father asked him to find out whether the fund would pay for a German grave instead of the flight. Murat said he would ask and never did.
- **Selin Yıldız** (46), the daughter, a social worker in Essen. *Only she knows:* sorting papers after the funeral, she found that Hasan reserved and paid for a two-place grave next to Ayşe in 2020, in his own name. She has not told Murat.
- **Elif Yıldız** (23), Murat's daughter, a trainee pharmacist with a toddler. *Only she knows:* two weeks before he died her grandfather said, "You'll come and talk to me, won't you? Bring the little one." He added that he was "tired of being a guest in both places." She has not dared tell her father.
- *Absent:* **Hasan Yıldız** (died aged 74). His brother **Ahmet** in the village (game-master character) insists Hasan "always said he'd come back to our parents."

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Where did Hasan want to be buried?**
- Readings: `village` (beside his parents); `beside_ayse` (in Duisburg, beside his wife); `left_to_children` (he had no fixed wish and left it to them); `home_to_die` (by "home" he meant: let me leave the hospital and die at home in Duisburg; it was not about burial at all).
- Evidence: 35 years of fund payments (S1) and his brother's claim → `village`; the grave reservation (S3), the question about the fund (S4) and the words to Elif (S5) → `beside_ayse`; a two-place reservation (S3) → perhaps meant for "family" rather than himself; morphine and the double meaning of *ev* (S6) → `home_to_die`; "tired of being a guest in both places" → any of them.
- Never settled: he left nothing in writing, his last words are ambiguous, and he died the next day.

## Interpretive conflict
Did Murat honour his father or overrule him? Was the speed a religious duty or a way to avoid discussion? Should the family consider exhumation and reburial, which some consider a disturbance of the dead? Does it matter more where Hasan wanted to lie, or who will visit him?

## Truth choices
- **Selin:** whether to show Murat the reservation. It would devastate him and may force talk of exhumation, which he would oppose.
- **Elif:** whether to tell her father what her grandfather said to her.
- **Murat:** whether to admit that his father asked him about a German grave in March.

## Neutral friend
**Kemal Arslan** (76), Hasan's workmate at the steelworks for 30 years and a fellow member of the mosque association. Murat calls him *amca*. He knew Ayşe and helped wash Hasan's body. He knows the public facts and the last words as Murat reports them.

## Values in tension
- Religious duty (prompt burial) against consulting the family
- An eldest son's authority against a daughter's knowledge
- Belonging and home for migrants: the village of origin or the place of the children
- Leaving the dead undisturbed against honouring their wish

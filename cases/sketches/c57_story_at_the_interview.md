# c57: The story at the interview

Kurdish (Sunni Muslim, from Qamishli, Syria), Malmö, Sweden, with an uncle in Germany and the father now in Iraqi Kurdistan (Middle East and diaspora) · family · departure (the father left and will not discuss the past), with destroyed records · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** drop. Risks implying refugee accounts are false; replaced by c65, where the institution's own error is central.

## Setting and culture
A Kurdish family that reached Sweden in 2015, among the many who fled Syria's war. Asylum rested partly on the father's account of being detained by state security in 2012. Swedish citizenship cannot be taken away, but many refugees still fear their old interviews. Family stories of flight are sacred, and they are owned by the head of the family. The fall of the Syrian regime in December 2024 opened prisons, but many records were burned. A daughter raised in Sweden thinks her story is hers to tell.

## The event
In 2025 Rojin Hassan signed a contract for a memoir about her family's flight, built around her father Ahmad's detention in 2012. Ahmad left the family in 2024, moved to Iraqi Kurdistan and remarried, and he will not discuss the past. In August 2026, interviewing her uncle Kawa for the book, Rojin heard him say: "It was me they took in 2012, not your father. Ahmad told my story at the interview because he was the head of the family and I was still in Turkey." Her mother, Nesrin, insists Ahmad was detained too, because she saw his wounds. When Rojin rang her father, he said only "Don't write about me" and hung up. The manuscript is due in January.

## People
- **Rojin Hassan** (26), social-work student and author. *Only she knows:* she obtained her father's 2015 asylum interview transcript for the book. Its details, a cell number and a guard's nickname, match exactly what Kawa described as his own.
- **Nesrin Hassan** (52), her mother, a school-kitchen worker. *Only she knows:* she saw marks on Ahmad's back in 2012, but he was gone for three days, not the three weeks he described at the interview.
- **Kawa Hassan** (55), Ahmad's younger brother, in Bielefeld, Germany. *Only he knows:* he himself was held for 23 days in 2012. That same month Ahmad was also held overnight at a checkpoint, and Kawa has not mentioned it.
- *Departed:* **Ahmad Hassan** (58), in Erbil, refusing to discuss it.

## Interpretive conflict
Whose suffering is it? Is borrowing a brother's story to save a family a lie, or a kind of truth? Does a daughter's book betray her parents, or honour them? Does the father's silence give him the last word?

## Truth choices

- **Nesrin:** whether to tell Rojin about the three days.
- **Kawa:** whether to tell Rojin about the checkpoint night, which partly restores his brother's claim.

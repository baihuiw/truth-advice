# c56: The tablets in the vitamin bottle

Bengali Hindu (middle class), Kolkata, India (South Asia) · partners (a young marriage and both families) · death · high stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
In Kolkata's middle-class arranged marriages, illness is often hidden. Epilepsy in particular carries stigma, and families conceal it to protect a daughter's chances. When a young wife dies within seven years of marriage, Indian law requires an inquest and allows a presumption against the husband's family if cruelty over dowry is shown. Families of brides sometimes file such cases in grief or anger. Honour on both sides is at stake.

## The event
Ishita, a schoolteacher with epilepsy controlled by medication since she was fourteen, married Sayan Basu in an arranged marriage in February 2025. Her family did not tell his family about the epilepsy. On 11 May 2026 she was found dead in bed at her in-laws' flat. The post-mortem showed no injuries and gave "probable seizure-related sudden death". The toxicology sample was reported insufficient for testing. Her mother, Mitali, believes the in-laws neglected her, or pushed her off her medicine, and is weighing a police complaint. Sayan's family says they knew nothing of her epilepsy until they found her pills in a vitamin bottle in March.

## People
- **Sayan Basu** (31), the husband, an IT engineer. *Only he knows:* he found the pills in March, felt betrayed, and spoke of divorce. After the wedding his father had asked Ishita's family for a car "as a gift". If that came out, it would support a dowry case.
- **Mitali Sen** (55), Ishita's mother, a retired bank officer. *Only she knows:* she told Ishita to keep the epilepsy hidden and to put the tablets in a vitamin bottle: "After the wedding they'll accept it." She also knows Ishita sometimes missed doses under stress, even before the marriage.
- **Moumita Sen** (24), Ishita's younger sister, a postgraduate student. *Only she knows:* on 2 May Ishita texted her: "I've stopped the tablets. They make me sleepy and Ma (mother-in-law) keeps asking about them. Don't tell Ma (our mother)."
- **Shyamali Basu** (58), the mother-in-law. *Only she knows:* in April she looked the pills up and told Ishita, "These are for fits? Our family doesn't have such things. Better you don't take them in front of guests."
- *Absent:* **Ishita Basu, née Sen** (died aged 27).

## Interpretive conflict
Whose concealment caused this: the mother's advice, the bride's choice, or the in-laws' stigma? Is a dowry complaint justice or revenge? Does the car demand matter to her death? Who carries the shame?

## Truth choices

- **Mitali:** whether to admit she advised concealment.
- **Shyamali:** whether to admit her remark.
- **Sayan:** whether to disclose the car request and his talk of divorce.

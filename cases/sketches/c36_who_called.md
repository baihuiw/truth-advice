# c36: Who called

Honduran and Mexican-American neighbours, an apartment complex in southwest Houston, Texas, USA (North America) · neighbours · death (the landlord, the one person who could say) · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A 1980s apartment complex of Central American and Mexican families, many of mixed immigration status, with children who are US citizens. Since 2025, immigration arrests at homes have increased, and neighbours whisper about tip lines and landlords who want tenants gone. Immigration authorities never say who tipped them, if anyone did. Churches and informal food businesses hold the community together, and suspicion can break it.

## The event
At dawn on 21 May 2026, immigration agents arrested Óscar Mejía, an undocumented roofer, outside his unit. They seemed to know his unit and his truck. He was deported in July. His wife Rosa and their three US-born children remain. People in the complex say someone called it in. Suspicion falls on the elderly landlord, Harold Kessler, who was trying to empty the building for renovations, and on a neighbour, Marisol Delgado, who had quarrelled with Óscar. Kessler died of a stroke in August. His son Kyle now manages the building and is selling it, and Rosa must decide whether to stay.

## People
- **Rosa Mejía** (35), Óscar's wife, a cleaner. *Only she knows:* Óscar had a 2019 drunk-driving arrest and an old deportation order issued in his absence. That may have made him a priority target without any tip. She has never told the neighbours.
- **Marisol Delgado** (61), the neighbour, a retired school-cafeteria manager and US citizen. *Only she knows:* in April, after a shouting match with Óscar over parking, she told Mr. Kessler at the leasing office, "That man doesn't even have papers and he's taking two spaces."
- **Kyle Kessler** (34), the landlord's son. *Only he knows:* in his father's desk notebook is a note dated 2 May: "Unit 14B — call about the illegals? (Méndez says ICE listens to landlords)." Nothing shows whether he called.
- *Absent:* **Harold Kessler** (died August 2026); **Óscar** (deported, in Honduras; he does not know either).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Did anyone tip off the agents about Óscar, and if so, who?**
- Readings: `landlord_tip` (Kessler called on his own); `remark_passed_on` (Kessler acted on Marisol's remark); `records_only` (no tip; Óscar was targeted from his old order); `someone_else` (another person called).
- Evidence: the notebook (S3) → `landlord_tip`; the remark (S2) → `remark_passed_on`; the old order (S1) → `records_only`; the agents knowing the truck → a tip, or vehicle records.
- Never settled: immigration authorities do not disclose sources, Kessler is dead, and Óscar's file does not say.

## Interpretive conflict
Is a careless remark a betrayal? What do neighbours owe each other in a raid? Was the landlord's note a plan or a passing thought? Does Rosa's silence about the old order wrong the neighbours she suspects?

## Truth choices
- **Marisol:** whether to confess her remark to Rosa.
- **Kyle:** whether to tell the tenants about his father's note, exposing his father and inviting a complaint.
- **Rosa:** whether to tell the neighbours about Óscar's record. It would clear them, but expose her family's past.

## Neutral friend
**Doña Chayo** (Rosario Vega, 70) runs the complex's tamale business and minds half its children. Mr. Kessler bought her tamales every Friday, and she has known Kyle since he was a boy. She is godmother to Rosa's youngest and plays lotería with Marisol. She knows the public facts and the whispers.

## Values in tension
- Neighbourly loyalty against careless talk
- Secrecy about status against clearing the innocent
- A son's loyalty to his dead father against tenants' right to know
- Protecting children against telling the truth

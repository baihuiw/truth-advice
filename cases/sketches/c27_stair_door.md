# c27: The stair door

Bengali (Muslim), a sweater factory in Gazipur, outside Dhaka, Bangladesh (South Asia) · work · death · high stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A garment factory of about 1,200 workers, mostly women from rural districts, in the industrial belt around Dhaka. After the disasters of 2012–13, fire-safety inspections tightened, yet some factories still lock side exits to stop theft or early leaving. Unions are weak, and organisers risk their jobs. When workers die, compensation depends on whether negligence is proven: brand-funded schemes pay more, and criminal cases follow. Before Eid, night overtime is routine.

## The event
At about 1 a.m. on 9 April 2026, during Eid overtime, a fire broke out in the fourth-floor finishing section. Most workers escaped by the main stairs. Four women died at the north stairwell door, which was found shut. The heat fused its padlock and buckled the door, so investigators could not say whether it was locked, jammed or blocked. The night guard, Abdul, who held the keys, died trying to help. The factory says the door was propped open and jammed in the fire. A criminal case, and the compensation tier for the families, now turn on that door.

## People
- **Rahima Khatun** (38), line supervisor in finishing. *Only she knows:* before Eid the admin manager told supervisors to keep the north door locked "to stop garments walking out", and she saw Abdul padlock it at 8 p.m. that night.
- **Jahid Mollah** (29), a knitter and secret union organiser. *Only he knows:* near midnight Abdul told him, "I have to lock the north door again; sir called." Jahid did not see whether he did. Jahid is gathering workers' accounts for the union.
- **Kamal Mia** (40), a rickshaw puller, widower of **Shirin**, one of the four. *Only he knows:* Shirin phoned him at 1:07 a.m.: "The door won't open! Kamal, the door..." and the line cut. He has told the police only that she called. He has also taken 200,000 taka "help" from the factory and signed a paper he cannot read.
- **Rezaul Karim** (50), the factory's admin manager. *Only he knows:* at 11:45 p.m. he phoned Abdul and said "keep the north door *bondho*", a word that can mean closed or locked, because inspectors might come early.
- *Absent:* **Abdul** the guard and the four women (died 9 April 2026).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Was the north door locked when the women reached it?**
- Readings: `locked` (padlocked); `jammed` (closed but unlocked, jammed by heat or warping); `blocked` (blocked by the stacked cartons, locked or not).
- Evidence: the 8 p.m. padlock (S2), "lock it again" (S5) and "keep it *bondho*" (S4) → `locked`; the routine unlocking at 10 p.m. (S3) → it may have been open for a while; "won't open" (S7) → any of the three; the cartons (S6) → `blocked`; the buckled door → `jammed`.
- Never settled: Abdul is dead, the padlock is fused, there was no camera on that stairwell, and none of the women at the door survived.

## Interpretive conflict
Negligence or accident? Is the guard to blame, or the manager who called him, or the order behind them both? Is accepting the factory's money a betrayal of the other families? Does solidarity among workers require testimony that costs jobs?

## Truth choices
- **Rezaul:** whether to tell investigators about his 11:45 call.
- **Rahima:** whether to testify that she saw the door locked at 8 p.m., risking her job and her section.
- **Jahid:** whether to give the union's case what Abdul said, which exposes his organising.
- **Kamal:** whether to tell the police Shirin's exact words, and the union what he signed.

## Neutral friend
**Ayesha Begum** (52) runs the tea stall at the factory gate. She knows every worker by name and rents Jahid a room. Rezaul stops for tea at her stall every morning, and Shirin often left her daughter with her after school. She knows the public facts and the rumours.

## Values in tension

- Loyalty to the employer against loyalty to co-workers

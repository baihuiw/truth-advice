# c46: The box behind the wardrobe

Cuban (Havana) and Cuban-American (Miami) · neighbours (across the revolution) · dementia · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
After 1959, the houses of families who emigrated were confiscated and assigned to others, often to loyal militia members. Neighbourhood committees watched over who might leave. Six decades on, exiles' grandchildren visit the houses their families lost, now let out as rooms by the families who received them. Restitution is a political matter, not a personal one, but grievance and gratitude are both live.

## The event
In 1962 the Arango family left Havana for Miami, and their house in Vedado was confiscated. In 1963 it was assigned to Mercedes Pérez, then a young militia member active in the neighbourhood committee. Mercedes, now 85, has advanced dementia. Her granddaughter Yanet runs a small guesthouse in the house. In October 2026 Daniel Arango, a Miami dentist and grandson of the original owners, came with his late grandmother's photos, and Yanet welcomed him. Looking at the photos, Mercedes said, "Clarita! I kept your things." Later she said, "I told them you were leaving." That week Yanet found a box behind the old wardrobe, holding the Arangos' photographs, letters and a gold medal of the Virgin.

## People
- **Yanet Pérez** (35), Mercedes's granddaughter, who runs the guesthouse. *Only she knows:* the box also holds a 1962 note from Clarita Arango to Mercedes: "Thank you for keeping our secret until we were gone." In 2015, before the dementia, Mercedes told her, "The Arangos were good people. I did what I had to do." Yanet has not yet shown Daniel the box.
- **Daniel Arango** (40), a dentist in Miami, Clarita's grandson. *Only he knows:* his grandmother told the story two ways. In the 1990s she said bitterly, "That *miliciana* took our house and denounced us." In 2018 she said, more softly, "Maybe she warned us; I never knew."
- *Unable to say:* **Mercedes Pérez** (85), with advanced dementia. **Clarita Arango** died in 2019.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Did Mercedes betray the Arangos, or protect them?**
- Readings: `denounced` (she reported their plan to leave, and that brought the confiscation); `protected` (she kept their secret until they were safely gone); `reported_after` (she told the authorities only once they were safe, to secure the house); `no_role` (she had no part; she was simply assigned the house).
- Evidence: "I told them you were leaving" (S6) → `denounced` or `reported_after`; the note thanking her for keeping their secret (S2) and the things kept for 63 years (S7) → `protected`; "I did what I had to do" (S5) → `reported_after` or `denounced`; Clarita's two versions (S4) → either way.
- Never settled: Mercedes has advanced dementia, Clarita is dead, and the committee's records are inaccessible or lost.

## Interpretive conflict
Revolutionary loyalty, or decency between neighbours? Can both have been true, in the order described by `reported_after`? Does the box belong to the Arangos, and do they deserve more than the box? Is an old woman's confused speech evidence?

## Truth choices
- **Yanet:** whether to show Daniel the box and the note, and whether to tell him what her grandmother said in 2015.
- **Daniel:** whether to tell Yanet about his grandmother's bitter version, or only the gentle one.

## Neutral friend
**Doña Fela** (Rafaela, 78) has lived across the street since 1960 and sells coffee from her window. She knew both families: she was a girl when the Arangos left and a friend of Mercedes for sixty years. Daniel has had coffee with her twice. She knows the public facts.

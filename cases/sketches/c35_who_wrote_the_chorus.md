# c35: Who wrote the chorus

Jamaican, Kingston's music scene (Caribbean) · friends (bandmates since youth) · death (with a lost record) · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
In 1990s Kingston, songs were made in small studios on handshakes. Credits often went to the singer or the label, and payments were cash "for now". A song from that era that is licensed decades later can suddenly earn real money, and old friends and heirs then fight over splits that were never written down. Trust, *respect*, and the dead's legacy carry great weight in the scene.

## The event
In 1996 the singer Winston "Jah Wins" Clarke and the guitarist Desmond Gayle recorded "Morning Rain" at a small Kingston studio. The song was registered to Clarke alone. Clarke died in 2019. In 2026 the song has been licensed for a global advertising campaign, and its royalties will be large. Desmond says he wrote the chorus hook and the guitar riff. Clarke's daughter Shanice, who runs the estate, says her father wrote it all. The producer's session notebook was lost when the studio flooded in 2004, and the engineer has died. Their lawyers are talking about a settlement.

## People
- **Desmond Gayle** (61), guitarist and session player. *Only he knows:* in 1996 Clarke gave him 20,000 Jamaican dollars "for the riff" and said, "A likkle something now, we sort out the rest later."
- **Shanice Clarke** (34), Winston's daughter, a bank officer and administrator of the estate. *Only she knows:* her father's 1996 diary says "Des come up with the hook. Sweet." The next line reads: "Fixed it so it's mine."
- *Absent:* **Winston Clarke** (died 2019).

## Interpretive conflict
Credit and fairness against a dead friend's legacy. Does thirty years of silence weaken Desmond's claim, or show his loyalty? Is Shanice honouring her father, or simply keeping the money? Do informal deals bind heirs?

## Truth choices

- **Shanice:** whether to disclose the diary, which says Desmond came up with the hook but also that her father "fixed it."

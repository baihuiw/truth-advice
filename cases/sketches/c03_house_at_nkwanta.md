# c03: The house at Nkwanta

Akan (Asante), a hometown near Kumasi, Ghana, and London (West Africa and diaspora) · family · death · ordinary stakes · 4 people + neutral friend · estate case

> **Status:** revise. Estate case whose choices are withholdings among kin; sharpen Kofi's partial quotation into a direct question from the family head.

## Setting and culture
The Asante are matrilineal. A man's lineage (*abusua*) runs through his mother and sisters, so his sister's son has a customary claim on what he leaves. A 1985 national law on intestate succession gave widows and children most of an estate, but lineage norms still shape practice, especially for houses built on family land. Funerals are large public events led by the family head. They are paid for by contributions and judged by how well they honour the dead. The diaspora in London builds houses "at home" over decades.

## The event
Kwadwo Asante, a retired London bus driver, spent twenty years building a two-storey house in his hometown, here called Nkwanta, on a plot his *abusua* gave him. Nkwanta ("crossroads") is a common Twi place name; no particular town is meant. His nephew Yaw supervised the building and has lived in two of its rooms since 2012. Kwadwo died suddenly in Croydon in February 2026, with no will. His widow, Akosua, and their son, Kofi, regard the house as theirs; Akosua planned to retire there. His elder sister Adwoa, the family head, says it is a family house. A letter from Kofi's solicitor about the national succession law has turned the dispute bitter. The final funeral rites in Nkwanta are set for December, and the family has said it will "decide the house after."

## People
- **Akosua Asante** (64), the widow, a retired NHS nurse in Croydon. *Only she knows:* £28,000 of the roof and wiring money came from her own pension lump sum. Kwadwo asked her not to tell his family that his wife had paid.
- **Kofi Asante** (36), their son, an accountant in London. *Only he knows:* the night before he died, Kwadwo told him in hospital: "The house is yours. But don't throw Yaw out."
- **Yaw Boateng** (47), Kwadwo's sister's son, who lives in Nkwanta and runs a phone shop. *Only he knows:* in 2024 Kwadwo asked him to find a surveyor to register the plot in Kwadwo's own name, and Yaw never arranged it.
- **Adwoa Boateng** (74), Kwadwo's elder sister, the family head, Yaw's mother. *Shares with Yaw (the London side do not know):* at the roofing thanksgiving in 2019, Kwadwo poured libation and said, "This house is for the *abusua*; let no one of our blood be homeless."
- *Absent:* **Kwadwo Asante** (died aged 69).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Kwadwo mean the house to be?**
- Readings: `abusua_house` (a family house for the lineage); `children_house` (his own property, for his wife and children); `both` (a family house in which his wife and children hold the main rooms); `retirement_home` (a home for himself and Akosua, with no thought beyond).
- Evidence: the libation (S4) and the *abusua* land (S1) → `abusua_house`; the surveyor request (S5) and Akosua's money (S3) → `children_house`; "The house is yours. But don't throw Yaw out" (S6) → `both`; the master bedroom finished to Akosua's choice → `retirement_home`.
- Never settled: he died suddenly, without a will. His statements were made to different people in different languages and settings, and none of them was written down.

## Interpretive conflict
Does what a man builds belong to his lineage or to his wife and children? Were Yaw's fourteen years of supervision a service or a stake? Does Akosua's hidden money give her a claim, and was it hers to hide? Who should pay for, and lead, a funeral that honours him?

## Truth choices
- **Akosua:** whether to break her promise to Kwadwo and reveal that her pension paid for the roof. It strengthens her claim but exposes what he wanted hidden from his family.
- **Kofi:** whether to report his father's last words in full. The second half ("don't throw Yaw out") weakens his own position, and he is tempted to quote only the first.
- **Yaw:** whether to mention the surveyor request, which cuts against the lineage claim.
- **Adwoa:** whether to report the libation as spoken or with her own emphasis, and whether to ask the London side what they know.

## Neutral friend
**Efua Mensah** (66), from Nkwanta, who has lived in Croydon since 1990. She goes to the same Methodist church as Akosua and is Kofi's godmother. She visits Nkwanta every Christmas and sits with Adwoa, and Yaw tops up her phone credit. She knows the public facts and the funeral plans.

## Values in tension
- Lineage property against the conjugal family
- A widow's unrecorded contribution against a husband's wish for discretion
- Honour (a fitting funeral) against cost and fairness
- Peace in the family against each side's full account

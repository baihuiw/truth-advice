# c23: The Sunday dinner

Polish (Catholic), Kraków (Central Europe) · partners (with the dead woman's family) · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend · estate-related (the flat)

## Setting and culture
Poland gives no legal status to same-sex couples. In July 2026 the president vetoed a bill for registered partnerships that would have covered access to medical information and decisions about burial. Since September 2026 Poland has recognised same-sex marriages contracted elsewhere in the EU. Without a will, the deceased's parents inherit and decide the funeral; a partner has no standing. Many people are out to friends and siblings but not to their parents.

## The event
Kasia Nowak, an architect, died of a brain aneurysm on 2 August 2026. For seven years she had lived with her partner Aga in a flat registered in Kasia's name; Aga paid half the mortgage, informally. Kasia's parents knew Aga as "Kasia's flatmate and best friend." At the hospital Aga was kept out of intensive care until Kasia's brother Paweł arrived and brought her in. Kasia's mother, Teresa, arranged a Catholic funeral, and Aga stood at the back. A week before she died, Kasia had asked Paweł to make sure their parents came to Sunday dinner on 9 August, because she had "something important to tell them." Teresa now plans to sell the flat, which means Aga must move out.

## People
- **Aga Wójcik** (36), Kasia's partner, a translator. *Only she knows:* they had booked a wedding at a registry office in Copenhagen for October and paid the deposit. On Kasia's laptop is an unsent, unfinished letter: "Mamo, I've wanted to tell you this for ten years..."
- **Teresa Nowak** (63), Kasia's mother, a retired bank clerk. *Only she knows:* in 2019 she found a birthday card in the flat reading "To my love, A.", put it back, and told herself it meant nothing.
- **Paweł Nowak** (31), Kasia's brother, a physiotherapist. *Only he knows:* Kasia came out to him in 2021 and he promised to keep it to himself. The week she died she told him, "I'm going to tell them everything on Sunday. If it goes badly, I need you on my side." In the same conversation she mentioned a job offer in Oslo.
- *Absent:* **Kasia Nowak** (died aged 34). Her father, **Jan**, is a game-master character.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Kasia mean to tell her parents on 9 August?**
- Readings: `relationship` (that she and Aga were a couple); `wedding` (that they were going to marry in Copenhagen); `job_abroad` (that she was taking the Oslo job); `several` (more than one of these).
- Evidence: "everything", "if it goes badly, I need you on my side" (S6) and "ten years" in the letter (S7) → `relationship`; the booking (S4) → `wedding`; the job offer (S6) → `job_abroad` or `several`.
- Never settled: she died a week before the dinner, and the letter breaks off after its first line.

## Interpretive conflict
Is Aga family? Was Teresa's silence about the card love, denial or fear? Does Paweł's promise of secrecy bind him once his sister is dead, given that she was about to tell their parents herself? Who owns the flat morally, as against legally?

## Truth choices
- **Paweł:** whether to tell his parents what Kasia told him, breaking his promise in order to carry out her plan.
- **Aga:** whether to tell Teresa about the wedding booking and the letter, and whether to ask for half the flat.
- **Teresa:** whether to admit she found the card years ago.

## Neutral friend
**Marta Zielińska** (34), Kasia's oldest school friend. She calls Teresa *ciocia* (auntie) and has had Sunday lunch at the Nowaks' for twenty years. She is friendly with Aga and knows Paweł well. She knows the public facts and that Aga "has nowhere to go."

## Values in tension
- The legal family against the chosen family
- The privacy of the dead against recognition for the living
- A promise to the dead against carrying out her intention
- Catholic family norms against love

# c29: Evaporated

Japanese, a small-workshop district of eastern Osaka (East Asia) · family · departure (a deliberate disappearance, *jōhatsu*) · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** drop. Open motive with little evidence either way; two users fear the same reading; modest truth choices.

## Setting and culture
Higashi-Osaka is a district of family-run metal workshops. Every year some people in Japan "evaporate" (*jōhatsu*): they leave on purpose to escape debt or shame, sometimes helped by "night-moving" firms. Police treat a voluntary adult disappearance as no crime and do not search. The shame of a failed family business falls on the whole household. A spouse who has been missing for three years can be grounds for divorce, and a court can declare death after seven.

## The event
Ishikawa Shin'ichi ran the metal-stamping workshop his father founded. On the night of 14 March 2025 he vanished, and neighbours saw a small van leave at 2 a.m. On the workbench he left a note: "I'm sorry. Don't look for me. Close the shop." The workshop owed 18 million yen. His wife, Keiko, had signed as guarantor, and the creditors now pursue her. In September 2026 a postcard with no return address, postmarked in Hokkaido, arrived at their son Daichi's flat in Kyoto: "I'm well. Forgive me." Keiko is deciding whether to begin a divorce, which needs a claim of desertion, and how to settle the debt.

## People
- **Ishikawa Keiko** (54), the wife, a part-time clerk. *Only she knows:* she learned of the debt in 2023 and urged him to go bankrupt, which he refused out of shame for his father's name. In February 2025 she told him, "If you can't fix this, I'll leave you."
- **Ishikawa Daichi** (27), their son, an office worker in Kyoto. *Only he knows:* two days before the disappearance his father phoned and asked, "If I weren't around, would you be all right?" Daichi said, "Don't be dramatic, Dad," and changed the subject. The postcard came to Daichi, and he has not shown it to his mother.
- *Absent:* **Ishikawa Shin'ichi** (57), whereabouts unknown, refusing contact.

## Interpretive conflict
Did he abandon his family or shield it? Was Keiko's ultimatum fair? Should Daichi honour "Don't look for me"? Is shame a reason, or an excuse?

## Truth choices

- **Keiko:** whether to tell Daichi about her ultimatum.

# c60: The house in Udon

Isaan Thai (Buddhist) and Norwegian, Udon Thani, Thailand, and Bergen, Norway (Southeast Asia and Northern Europe) · partners (a wife and her husband's daughter) · dementia · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
In northeast Thailand (Isaan), it is common for local women to marry older Western men. Foreigners cannot own land in Thailand, so houses they pay for are put in the Thai wife's name. A right of lifelong use, called a usufruct, can be registered to protect the foreigner, but often is not. Isaan norms expect children to support their parents, and the husband's money is expected to help the wife's family. Norwegian norms treat property as individual, and care for the old is paid for by the state and by savings. Each side readily suspects the other of taking advantage.

## The event
Arne Olsen, a retired ship's engineer, married Ploy Saengthong in 2014. He paid 2.4 million baht for land and a house in Udon Thani, registered in Ploy's name, with no usufruct. They lived there for ten years, and Ploy's mother lives with them. Arne was diagnosed with Alzheimer's in 2024. In 2025 his daughter Ingrid brought him back to Bergen for care; Ploy visits when she can. Ingrid now holds his power of attorney. She wants the house sold and half the proceeds put toward his care home. Ploy says the house was a gift to her and her mother.

## People
- **Ploy Saengthong** (44), Arne's wife, who ran a small salon in Udon. *Only she knows:* in 2019 Arne told her in English, "This is your house, for your mother and you, after me." Part of his Norwegian pension still arrives each month in their joint Thai account, and she sends some of it to her mother.
- **Ingrid Olsen** (45), Arne's daughter, a schoolteacher in Bergen. *Only she knows:* in 2021 her father emailed her: "The house is my pension. If something happens, sell it and take care of me." The same email says, "Ploy is a good woman; don't let anyone treat her badly." She has shown her lawyer only the first part.
- *Unable to say:* **Arne Olsen** (77), with advanced dementia, in a Bergen care home.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Arne mean the house to be?**
- Readings: `gift_to_ploy` (a gift to Ploy and her family); `his_security` (his pension, to be sold for his care); `their_home` (their shared home, with no thought beyond).
- Evidence: "your house... after me" (S2) → `gift_to_ploy`, though "after me" can mean only after his death; "the house is my pension" (S3) → `his_security`; "don't let anyone treat her badly" (S3) → protecting Ploy; the title in Ploy's name (S1) → the law required it, so no intention can be read from it.
- Never settled: his dementia is advanced, and nothing was put in writing between them.

## Interpretive conflict
A gift, or security for old age? Does ten years of care for him give Ploy a claim of her own? Is supporting a wife's mother a duty or exploitation? Is Ingrid protecting her father, or the inheritance?

## Truth choices
- **Ploy:** whether to tell Ingrid that the pension still arrives in Thailand, and where it goes.
- **Ingrid:** whether to show the whole email to Ploy and the lawyer, including "don't let anyone treat her badly."

## Neutral friend
**Wanida Hansen** (52), a Thai woman married to a Norwegian in Bergen. She met Ploy at the Thai temple in Bergen during Ploy's visits and became her friend. She has helped Ingrid by interpreting at the care home. She knows the public facts and that "there is a lawyer now."

## Values in tension
- A gift against security in old age
- Family obligations across cultures against individual property
- Gratitude for care against suspicion of exploitation
- Holding back half a document against fair dealing

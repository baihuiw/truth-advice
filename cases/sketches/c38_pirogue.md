# c38: The pirogue

Wolof and Serer (Sunni Muslim), the fishing town of Mbour, Senegal (West Africa) · friends (with the missing man's mother) · disappearance (lost at sea, never confirmed) · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Mbour, on the Petite Côte, has emptying fish stocks, idle young men, and wooden pirogues that leave at night for the Canary Islands. Families pay smugglers, and some mothers secretly help. Boats vanish; survivors' accounts come back in pieces. Without a body, a family can pray the funeral prayer for the absent, but some fathers refuse, because it means giving up. Friends who stayed behind carry a heavy guilt.

## The event
On the night of 29 August 2025, two pirogues left a beach near Mbour for the Canaries. Ibrahima Diouf (22) was meant to be on the larger one. His best friend, Moussa Sarr, was meant to go too, but backed out at the last moment after his mother's pleading. Before leaving, Ibrahima gave Moussa 300,000 CFA francs "to keep for my mother if anything happens". In September about forty survivors of the larger boat were rescued near Cape Verde; more than a hundred were lost. Two survivors say they saw Ibrahima fall overboard in a storm. A third says he had moved to the smaller boat, which has never been found. A year on, his mother Aminata wants to hold the prayer for the absent, and his father refuses.

## People
- **Moussa Sarr** (23), Ibrahima's best friend, an unemployed fisherman. *Only he knows:* the night before, Ibrahima said he might switch to the smaller boat, because its captain was a friend of his cousin. Moussa has also spent 100,000 of the 300,000 CFA francs on his own mother's medicine.
- **Aminata Ndiaye** (47), Ibrahima's mother, a fish-smoker. *Only she knows:* she secretly gave Ibrahima 200,000 CFA francs towards the crossing, which her husband had forbidden. At 2 a.m. that night Ibrahima called her: "*Yaay*, I'm on the big pirogue, pray for me."
- *Disappeared:* **Ibrahima Diouf** (22).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Did Ibrahima die at sea?**
- Readings: `drowned_big_boat` (lost overboard from the larger boat); `small_boat` (he was on the smaller boat, whose fate is unknown); `alive_silent` (he survived somewhere and has not called).
- Evidence: the two survivors (S6) → `drowned_big_boat`; the third survivor and the talk of switching (S2, S6) → `small_boat`; "I'm on the big pirogue" (S4) → `drowned_big_boat`, though "big" depends on who is speaking; a year of silence (S7) → death, or a choice not to call.
- Never settled: there is no body and no passenger list, the smaller boat vanished, and survivors' memories conflict.

## Interpretive conflict
Hope or closure? Does Moussa owe the whole sum, now that part has gone on his own mother? Is a mother's secret help a betrayal of her husband, or a mercy to her son? Who is to blame: the smugglers, the families, the empty sea?

## Truth choices
- **Moussa:** whether to give Aminata the money, admit what he spent, and tell her about the smaller boat. That would give her hope that may be false.
- **Aminata:** whether to tell her husband about the money she gave and the 2 a.m. call, which supports the prayer he refuses.

## Neutral friend
**Tonton Ablaye Ndiaye** (60), the neighbourhood tailor. He sewed Ibrahima's festival clothes every year, and the young men drink tea under his awning. He is a friend of Aminata's husband and has known Moussa since birth. He knows the public facts and the survivors' accounts.

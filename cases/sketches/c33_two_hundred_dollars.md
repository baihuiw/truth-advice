# c33: Two hundred dollars

White Appalachian (Baptist), a coal town in southern West Virginia, USA (North America) · family · death (overdose) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** keep.

## Setting and culture
A hollow town where the mines have closed and the opioid crisis has run through nearly every family. Fentanyl now taints most street pills. Recovery groups, churches and naloxone kits are part of daily life, and families argue about whether giving money to an addicted relative is love or enabling. People rarely ask for help, and shame is heavy.

## The event
Cody Rakes had been in recovery for fourteen months. On 14 July 2026 he was found dead of a fentanyl overdose in his truck behind the Dollar store where his mother works. The day before, his mother Darlene had given him $200 in cash "for his truck payment." His girlfriend Kayla found him; by then naloxone was too late. His sister Brandi had begged their mother never to give Cody cash, and she has barely spoken to Darlene since the funeral. Darlene now asks anyone who will listen whether her money killed her son.

## People
- **Darlene Rakes** (56), Cody's mother, a store clerk. *Only she knows:* the $200 was not a one-off. She had been giving him $50 to $100 a week for months.
- **Kayla Sizemore** (27), Cody's girlfriend, a home health aide. *Only she knows:* Cody relapsed two weeks earlier; she found foil in the truck, and he begged her not to tell his mother. She moved out after a fight that week. When the police returned his wallet to her, it held $340, more than the $200, because he had been paid cash for a roofing job that day.
- **Brandi Rakes** (33), Cody's sister, a school bus driver. *Only she knows:* on 12 July Cody asked her for money. She refused and said, "You're going to kill Mom one day." It was the last thing she said to him.
- *Absent:* **Cody Rakes** (died aged 29).

# c54: The lease at the river bend

Indo-Fijian (Hindu) cane farmers and an iTaukei landowning clan, the western cane belt of Viti Levu, Fiji, with the uncle in Canada (Oceania and diaspora) · family (with the landowning clan) · departure (the uncle refuses all contact) · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend · estate case (a lease)

## Setting and culture
Most cane land in Fiji is native land owned by iTaukei clans (*mataqali*). Indo-Fijian farmers lease it for 30 years through a national land trust. Since the late 1990s many leases have expired without renewal, and the coups of 1987 and 2000 drove many Indo-Fijian families abroad. Renewal depends on the clan's goodwill and on the lessees' paperwork. Ties between farmers and clans can be close, built on years of reciprocity, or tense.

## The event
In 1996 the brothers Ram and Suresh Prasad leased ten acres from a *mataqali*. After the 2000 coup, Suresh emigrated to Canada. In 2003 he signed a paper transferring "my interest to my brother Ram for safekeeping until I return." The paper was never registered, and the lawyer's files were lost in a 2012 flood. Ram died in 2009. At his funeral the brothers' families quarrelled, and Suresh has refused all contact with Fiji since. The lease expires in December 2026. The clan has agreed in principle to renew it to Ram's son Ravin, but the land trust needs every registered lessee to consent. Suresh's daughter Anjali, who holds his power of attorney, has written asking for half the value of the farm.

## People
- **Ravin Prasad** (52), Ram's son, who farms the land. *Only he knows:* his father's 2004 diary reads: "Sent Suresh $5,000. He says now the farm is mine. Thank God. But he cried on the phone; I don't think he means it."
- **Anjali Prasad-Sharma** (40), Suresh's daughter, a bookkeeper in Surrey, British Columbia. *Only she knows:* in 2020 her father told her, "I signed away the farm for my brother's sake; don't ever fight Ravin over it." Her own family's finances are in trouble since her husband lost his job.
- **Josefa Vakaloloma** (58), the clan's spokesman for the lease. *Only he knows:* the clan elders are split, and some want the land for a tourism project. They agreed to renew only "if the lessee family is united." If the dispute runs past December, they may let the lease lapse.
- *Departed:* **Suresh Prasad** (70), in Vancouver, who says only "Don't talk to me about Fiji." *Absent:* **Ram Prasad** (died 2009).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Suresh mean by the 2003 transfer?**
- Readings: `gave_up` (he gave his share to Ram for good, and "safekeeping" was politeness); `in_trust` (he kept his interest, which Ram held for him); `bought_out` (Ram's $5,000 bought his share).
- Evidence: "for safekeeping until I return" (S3) → `in_trust`; "now the farm is mine" (S4) and "I signed away the farm" (S7) → `gave_up` or `bought_out`; "he cried... I don't think he means it" (S4) → `in_trust`; twenty years without a claim → `gave_up`.
- Never settled: Suresh refuses to speak of it, Ram is dead, and the lawyer's files are lost.

## Interpretive conflict
A brother's sacrifice, or a share held in trust? Does an emigrant who left keep a claim on land he never worked? Is Anjali acting for her father or against his wishes? Do the clan's owners have a stake in the brothers' quarrel, or only in the outcome?

## Truth choices
- **Ravin:** whether to show the diary. Its first line helps him and its second hurts him.
- **Anjali:** whether to disclose her father's 2020 words, which undercut her own letter.
- **Josefa:** whether to warn both sides that the clan may let the lease lapse if they keep fighting.

## Neutral friend
**Kamla Wati** (68) runs the settlement's shop. She knew Ram and Suresh as boys, sells kava to Josefa's village for ceremonies, and put Anjali up in 2018, when she visited. She knows the public facts and the expiry date.

# c42: Plot 47

East German and Syrian refugee neighbours, an allotment colony in Leipzig, Germany (Europe) · community (allotment-garden association) · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Allotment gardens (*Kleingärten*) are rented from a members' association, which has strict rules: a third of the plot for fruit and vegetables, a small hut, and no living there. In cities, waiting lists run for years, and the board, not the outgoing tenant, chooses the next one. Associations are proud of their rules. Some have been accused of keeping out "foreigners", while others celebrate their new members. In Leipzig, older East German members and refugee families who arrived in 2015–16 now garden side by side.

## The event
Günter Haase, a retired tram driver, held plot 47 for 38 years. For his last three years he shared it with the Al-Hamwi family, Syrian refugees: Rania and her two children weeded, harvested and sat with him in the hut. He called them "my second family." In spring 2026 he told Rania, "The garden will be yours; I'm putting it in writing to the board." He died in May. The chairman, Herr Zimmer, says no request was received, and the plot goes to the next couple on the waiting list. His daughter Heike, clearing his flat, found a signed, dated letter to the board asking for the transfer, in a stamped envelope that was never sealed or posted. The board meets on 15 November.

## People
- **Rania Al-Hamwi** (39), a nursery assistant. *Only she knows:* Günter gave her a key and paid her children pocket money for weeding. That is informal sub-letting, which is against the rules. After a shouting match with Herr Zimmer in March about her family's presence, Günter told her, "Don't worry; I'll fix it."
- **Heike Haase** (54), Günter's daughter, a pharmacist in Munich. *Only she knows:* the unposted letter. In April her father also told her, "I don't want the club fighting over me; maybe it's better if the Syrians aren't dragged into it."
- **Herr Klaus Zimmer** (68), chairman of the association. *Only he knows:* in April Günter phoned him to say, "I'll write to you about Rania." In 2025 the board voted in private to tighten rules on non-members gardening, after a neighbour's complaints about the Al-Hamwis. Zimmer voted against, and as chairman he must now enforce the rule.
- *Absent:* **Günter Haase** (died aged 81).

## Permanently unknowable

## Interpretive conflict
Rules and fairness to the waiting list, or a dead man's kindness? Does informal sharing create a claim? Is the association enforcing order, or prejudice? Should an unposted letter count?

## Truth choices
- **Heike:** whether to give the board the letter. It is formally invalid but morally weighty, and it will start the fight her father wanted to avoid.
- **Zimmer:** whether to tell the board about Günter's phone call.
- **Rania:** whether to admit the key and the payments. They show the bond, and they also break the rules.

# c20: Forty-four years

> **Status:** revise. The unknowable is lopsided (after 44 years death is near certain); recast Georges's choice as a lie to spare Samira when she asks him directly.

## Setting and culture
About 17,000 people disappeared in Lebanon's civil war (1975–90): taken at checkpoints, some rumoured to have ended up in Syrian prisons. A 1995 law lets families have a missing person declared dead after four years. Many mothers and wives refused, seeing it as a betrayal. A 2018 law promised to search for the missing but has achieved little. Families hold sit-ins with photographs. Land in mountain villages passes through the male line, and today emigration drains the young.

## The event
Antoine Haddad was stopped at a militia checkpoint near Beirut in September 1982 and never seen again. His wife, Samira, has never had him declared dead; she carries his photo to every sit-in. Their son Elie, born in 1981, wants to emigrate to Canada. To pay for it he wants to sell the family's olive terraces, which are registered in Antoine's name, and the land registry needs a death declaration. Samira refuses to sign. Antoine's brother Georges owns the neighbouring terraces and has stayed out of it. The developer's offer runs out at the end of the year.

## People
- **Samira Haddad** (70), Antoine's wife, a retired seamstress. *Only she knows:* in 1991 a man came to her door saying he had been released from a prison in Syria and had seen "an Antoine from the Metn" alive there in 1988. She paid him $2,000 for more news, and he never came back. She has told no one, for fear of looking a fool.
- **Elie Haddad** (45), their son, a surveyor. *Only he knows:* he has already signed a preliminary agreement with a developer and spent part of the deposit.
- **Georges Haddad** (74), Antoine's younger brother, a retired schoolteacher. *Only he knows:* in 2001 a former militiaman told him in confidence that Antoine was shot at the checkpoint that same night and buried near the river. Georges never told Samira, because he could not prove it and did not want to take away her hope.
- *Absent:* **Antoine Haddad** (taken in 1982, aged 31).

## Interpretive conflict
Is declaring death a betrayal, or a mercy for the living? Was Georges's 25-year silence mercy or cowardice? May a son sell what was his father's when his mother still waits? Is Samira's hope faith or a refusal to grieve?

## Truth choices
- **Georges:** whether to tell Samira and Elie what the militiaman told him, which could end her hope.
- **Samira:** whether to tell them about the 1991 visitor, which would feed Elie's fear that his father suffered for years.
- **Elie:** whether to tell his mother that he has already signed and spent part of the deposit.

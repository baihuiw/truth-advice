# c22: The night of the horses

Khalkh Mongolian herders, Arkhangai province, Mongolia (Central/East Asia) · family · memory loss (the father survived with a brain injury and no memory of that night) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A herding household in the steppe of Arkhangai. In a *dzud* (a killing winter of deep snow and cold), whole herds can die. Moving the animals to distant pasture (*otor*) can save them, but it means months away from home, school and work. Animals belong to individual family members but are herded together. Horses are a man's pride. The father heads the household, and grown sons are expected to obey him and to share the work.

## The event
In the 2025–26 *dzud* the Dorj family's combined herd lost more than half its animals. On the night of 18 February 2026, in a blizzard, the father, Dorjsuren, rode out alone to find the horse herd that had drifted, without waking his younger son, who slept in the next *ger*. He was found at dawn, unconscious, with severe hypothermia. He survived weeks in the provincial hospital with a brain injury. He remembers nothing of that night or the weeks before, and he speaks with difficulty. The forty horses, his pride, were never found. Now the family must decide how to divide the surviving animals between households, since the father can no longer herd.

## People
- **Bat-Erdene** (38), the elder son, a mechanic in the district centre with a wife and a schoolboy son. *Only he knows:* in November his father asked him to take the herd on *otor* to a neighbouring province where there was grass. He refused because of his son's school and his wife's job, and said "next week", but never went.
- **Ganbold** (33), the younger son, who lives with his parents and herds. *Only he knows:* in December he sold two tonnes of the family's hay to a neighbour for cash to repay a debt, and told his father it had spoiled. The hay ran out early in February.
- **Oyuntsetseg** (60), the mother. *Only she knows:* that night, before he went out, she told her husband, "Your sons don't respect you, and you've lost everything." He went out without a word, and she did not wake Ganbold.
- *Unable to tell:* **Dorjsuren** (64), alive, with no memory of that night.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Why did Dorjsuren ride out alone that night?**
- Readings: `save_horses` (simply to bring in the horses before they died); `pride` (to show his sons he could still do it alone); `stung` (driven out by his wife's words); `clouded` (cold and exhaustion clouded his judgement); `despair` (he did not much care whether he came back).
- Evidence: the drifted horses (S5) → `save_horses`; his wife's words (S4) → `stung` or `despair`; his remark to a neighbour that winter, "my sons are herders on telephones" → `pride`; the blizzard and his exhaustion → `clouded`.
- Never settled: he cannot remember and is unlikely ever to, and only his wife saw him leave.

**U2. Would *otor* in November have saved most of the herd?**
- Readings: `saved` (most of the herd would have lived); `partly` (fewer losses, still heavy); `no` (the distant pasture also failed, or the trek would have killed as many).
- Evidence: the neighbours' smaller losses (S8) → `saved` or `partly`; the one family that lost as much → `no`.
- Never settled: it is a counterfactual about one winter that cannot be rerun.

## Interpretive conflict
Who failed the herd: the son who refused *otor*, the son who sold the hay, the mother's words, or the winter itself? Was the night ride courage or pride? Since the father's own animals died most, should his sons give him some of theirs?

- **All:** what to tell Dorjsuren himself when he asks why he went out.

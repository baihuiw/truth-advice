# c02: The knock through the wall

Japanese, a public housing estate in Tohoku (East Asia) · neighbours · death (isolated death, found after nine days) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A 1970s public housing estate (*danchi*) on the edge of a fictional Tohoku city, Kitase. Most residents are elderly and live alone. Norms of restraint (*enryo*) and of not causing trouble (*meiwaku*) make people slow to knock on a neighbour's door. Volunteer welfare commissioners keep a loose watch over the old. An isolated death (*kodokushi*) brings shame to the son and to the building. The 49th-day memorial, when the bones are interred, is coming up.

## The event
Kudo Isamu (81), a widower, lived alone in unit 3-204. In late August 2026 he collapsed in his bathroom. He was found nine days later, when the downstairs resident reported a smell. The police doctor gave heart failure as the cause and estimated the death at between 21 and 25 August; there was no autopsy. His only son, Takeshi, lives near Tokyo; they had argued in July about a care facility and had not spoken since. Next door, Sato Fumiko heard something in the night and did nothing, and the estate's volunteer commissioner saw newspapers piling up and assumed Kudo had gone away. The residents' association is now writing a "lessons" report. Takeshi has invited both of them to the 49th-day memorial.

## People
- **Kudo Takeshi** (52), the son, a sales manager in Saitama. *Only he knows:* in May, at his father's insistence, he cancelled the estate's daily watch-over phone call. After the July argument he did not ring for five weeks.
- **Sato Fumiko** (76), the next-door neighbour of 30 years. *Only she knows:* around 1 a.m. on 22 August she heard a heavy thud and three weak knocks through the shared bathroom wall. Her husband said not to make a fuss, and she went back to sleep.
- **Ono Shigeru** (68), the estate's volunteer welfare commissioner. *Only he knows:* he noticed the newspapers on about 24 August and did not check. Kudo had told him he "might go to Takeshi's for a few days."
- *Absent:* **Kudo Isamu** (died aged 81).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Was Isamu still alive after the thud, and could he have been saved?**
- Readings: `instant` (he died at or very near the fall, and nothing could have been done); `hours` (alive for some hours, and someone going in that night could have saved him); `days` (alive for a day or more, so the cancelled daily call would have found him); `unrelated` (the thud was something else, and he died at another time).
- Evidence: the knocks after the thud → `hours`; the doctor's range running to 25 August → `days` is possible; the bathroom light on and dinner untouched → `instant` or `hours`; his heart history → `instant`.
- Never settled: there was no autopsy and decomposition was advanced; no one saw or heard anything more.

**U2. Did Isamu really mean to visit his son?**
- Readings: `meant_visit` (he planned to go and make it up); `excuse` (he said it to keep the commissioner from checking on him); `idle` (a passing remark).
- Evidence: the gift-wrapped sweets two days later → `meant_visit`; no ticket was found and he never called Takeshi → `excuse` or `idle`.
- Never settled: he told no one else.

## Interpretive conflict
Who failed him: the son who stopped the calls and stayed silent, the neighbour who heard and stayed in bed, the volunteer who saw the papers, or nobody, if he died at once? Is restraint a courtesy or a neglect? Did Isamu himself choose this solitude?

## Truth choices
- **Sato:** whether to tell Takeshi, at or before the memorial, what she heard that night. She fears blame, and she fears what it would do to him.
- **Takeshi:** whether to tell the association that the daily call was cancelled at his request. He has been privately blaming the estate.
- **Ono:** what to write in his account: the date he noticed the papers, and his assumption.

## Neutral friend
**Ito Haruko** (73) runs the small grocery at the estate entrance. She sold Isamu his tofu for thirty years, chats with Sato every morning and with Ono at the residents' meetings. Takeshi used to ring her for news of his father. She knows the public facts and has heard bits from all three.

## Values in tension
- Respect for privacy against a duty to check on the vulnerable
- A child's obligation to a parent who rejects help against the parent's wish for independence
- Sparing a grieving son against his right to know how his father died
- Collective watch against individual responsibility

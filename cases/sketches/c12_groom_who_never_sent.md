# c12: The groom who never sent for her

Jat Sikh, a village in the Doaba region of Punjab, India, and Brampton, Canada (South Asia and diaspora) · partners (with both families) · departure · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Doaba is a heartland of emigration. Marriage to a man settled in Canada is a prize, and families borrow heavily for weddings, gold and a car. "Abandoned brides", women married to men abroad who never send for them, are a recognised social problem. A family's honour (*izzat*) rests on a daughter's marriage, and a broken one shames both families. Divorce means serving papers on a man who may be impossible to find.

## The event
Harleen Kaur married Jaspreet Singh Sandhu, a truck driver in Brampton on a work permit, in February 2024. Her father spent about 32 lakh rupees, much of it borrowed. Jaspreet left after three weeks, promising to sponsor her once his permanent residence came through. His calls thinned through 2024 and stopped in mid-2025, and in October 2025 his number went dead. His parents say they do not know where he is. Harleen's father has filed a police complaint and is pressing the groom's family to return the gold and money. Harleen, now 27, is thinking about divorce, and the two families no longer speak.

## People
- **Harleen Kaur** (27), schoolteacher, the bride. *Only she knows:* in January 2025 Jaspreet told her on a call, "Don't wait for me if I can't fix the papers." She never told her father.
- **Balwinder Singh** (58), farmer, her father. *Only he knows:* he paid a go-between 5 lakh rupees for "assurance" about the groom. He knew Jaspreet's residence was only "in process", but told the village it had been granted. Two years before the match he had forbidden Harleen's wish to marry a classmate from another caste.
- **Simran Kaur Sandhu** (29), the groom's sister, a nurse in the next village. *Only she knows:* her brother's residence application was refused in August 2024, and he owed money to an immigration consultant. He had a girlfriend in Canada before the wedding whom he said he'd left. In 2026 he messaged her twice from a US number: "Tell Ma I'm alive. Don't tell anyone where."
- *Absent:* **Jaspreet Singh Sandhu** (31), somewhere in the United States, refusing contact.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Did Jaspreet ever mean to bring Harleen to Canada?**
- Readings: `scheme` (no; he married for the dowry, or to satisfy his family); `failed_in_good_faith` (yes; he meant to, his status collapsed, and shame made him vanish); `changed_mind` (at first, then he chose another life, perhaps the old girlfriend); `pushed_by_family` (his parents pushed the match to pay his consultant debt, and he went along).
- Evidence: "Don't wait for me if I can't fix the papers" (S5) → `failed_in_good_faith`; the refusal and the debt (S4) → `failed_in_good_faith` or `pushed_by_family`; the prior girlfriend → `scheme` or `changed_mind`; the slow fade of the calls (S6) → `changed_mind`.
- Never settled: he refuses all contact and will never explain, and his family's accounts serve themselves.

## Interpretive conflict
Fraud, or a tragedy of immigration rules? Whose honour has been damaged, and by whom? Did Balwinder's pride, and his refusal of Harleen's own choice, set this up? Is the groom's family liable for his flight? Is Harleen free?

## Truth choices
- **Simran:** whether to tell Harleen that Jaspreet is alive and in the US. It would help her divorce and betray her brother and mother.
- **Harleen:** whether to tell her father about "don't wait for me". It would soften the fraud story he needs for the police case.
- **Balwinder:** whether to admit he knew the residence was not granted.

## Neutral friend

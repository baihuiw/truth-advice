# c08: The written wish

Dutch, a town in Utrecht province, Netherlands (Europe) · care and medical · dementia (followed by death) · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** keep.

## Setting and culture
Dutch law allows a doctor to act on a written advance euthanasia directive for dementia. In practice doctors are very cautious, and professional guidance stresses that the doctor must be able to communicate with the patient. Short of euthanasia, the family and the care-home physician can decide to give comfort care only, for example no antibiotics for pneumonia. A spouse acting as legal representative carries that decision. The family is secular with a Protestant background; directness and consensus are both prized.

## The event
Pieter de Vries, a retired history teacher, signed an advance directive in 2020, soon after his Alzheimer's diagnosis. It says that when he no longer recognises his wife Marijke he wants euthanasia. By early 2026 he did not know her, but in the care home he seemed content: he sang, and he held hands with another resident. The GP declined euthanasia; asked, Pieter said "*nee, nee*." In August 2026 he developed pneumonia. Marijke, as his representative, asked for comfort care only, citing the directive and their old talks, and the care-home physician agreed. Their daughter Anouk objected by phone. Pieter died a week later. Anouk has now asked the home for his full night notes, and the home has asked the night carer for a statement.

## People
- **Marijke de Vries** (75), the widow, a retired music teacher. *Only she knows:* in 2019, on a walk, Pieter said, "If I become a stranger to you, let me go. Don't let them keep me going with pills." *Partly known to Anouk:* since 2025 Marijke has had a close friendship with Jan, a widower from her choir.
- **Anouk de Vries** (46), daughter, a policy officer in Utrecht. *Only she knows:* in 2021 Pieter told her, "If I seem happy, let me be." She has seen messages between her mother and Jan and suspects more than friendship, but has said nothing.
- **Fatima El Amrani** (40), the night-shift carer at the home. *Only she knows:* for months Pieter woke at 3 a.m. several nights a week, frightened and crying for his mother. The night notes said only "restless, settled with reassurance." On his last night he said, "*Mam, ik kom*" ("Mum, I'm coming").
- *Absent:* **Pieter de Vries** (died aged 79).

## Interpretive conflict
Do past wishes bind a changed person? Did Marijke act from love, from exhaustion, or, as Anouk fears, from her new attachment? Did the home's thin notes hide suffering that would have settled the family's argument? Did Anouk's objection protect her father, or prolong his fear?

# c11: Sunday lunch, March 2020

Lombard Italian and a Ukrainian live-in carer, a town in the Seriana valley near Bergamo (Europe) · family · death (COVID-19, 2020) · ordinary stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** keep.

## Setting and culture
A small valley town in the province of Bergamo, one of the places hit first and worst in spring 2020. In those weeks the old died in hospital without visitors, and funerals were banned. Many families employ a live-in *badante*, often an Eastern European woman; in 2020 many were undocumented, and an amnesty that May let employers regularise them. The family is Catholic. The anniversary Mass for the dead is a fixed point of the year.

## The event
On Sunday 1 March 2020 the family ate lunch at Nonna Rosa's house: her son Giorgio, his wife Daniela, their son Luca, and Oksana, who lived with Rosa as her carer. Ten days later Rosa had a fever. She died alone in hospital in Bergamo on 24 March, one of thousands that month. For six years the family said nobody could have known. At the sixth-anniversary Mass in March 2026, Luca's former girlfriend told Giorgio that Luca had had a fever that weekend. Giorgio has not spoken to his son since. Each of the four has a reason to fear that it was they who brought it in.

## People
- **Luca Bonfanti** (35), grandson, a software developer in Milan. *Only he knows:* he had a temperature of 38°C on 29 February and 1 March, took paracetamol, and came to lunch.
- **Daniela Bonfanti** (62), daughter-in-law, a nurse at the hospital in Bergamo. *Only she knows:* on 27–28 February she nursed two patients with "atypical pneumonia", later known to be COVID. On 1 March she had a sore throat and hugged Rosa.
- **Oksana Hrytsenko** (58), Rosa's live-in carer from 2017 to 2020, then undocumented; regularised in 2020 with Giorgio's sponsorship. *Only she knows:* she had a cough and fever from 28 February and hid it to keep her job and her bed.
- **Giorgio Bonfanti** (64), Rosa's son, a retired plumber. *Only he knows:* on 26 February he took Rosa to a crowded card evening at the bocce club, where two men later fell ill. He had promised Daniela to keep his mother at home.
- *Absent:* **Rosa Bonfanti** (died aged 88).

- Evidence: every one of S1 to S5; the 10 March onset fits all of them; in the valley that week, almost everyone was exposed.
- Never settled: there was no testing outside hospital then, Rosa's virus was never sequenced, and none of the others was tested.

## Interpretive conflict
Is there blame at all in a pandemic nobody understood yet? Is hiding a fever worse when you need the job to stay in the country? Did Giorgio's anger at Luca come from grief, or from his own unspoken evening at the club? Should anyone confess something that can never be proved?

## Truth choices
- **Luca:** whether to admit the fever to his father, or to say his ex is exaggerating.
- **Daniela:** whether to tell the family about the ward and her sore throat. It would shift blame from her son onto herself.
- **Oksana:** whether to tell the family she was ill. She is now regularised, but she still depends on their goodwill and references.
- **Giorgio:** whether to admit the club evening while he blames his son.

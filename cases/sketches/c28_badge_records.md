# c28: The badge records

Taiwanese (Hokkien-speaking family), Hsinchu Science Park, Taiwan (East Asia) · work · lost record (with the worker's death) · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Hsinchu's semiconductor-equipment firms run on long hours and loyalty, and many engineers work late into the night at home. For a death to count as caused by overwork, the Ministry of Labor's guidelines require proof of heavy overtime before the illness: over 100 hours in the final month, or a high average over the months before. Families must prove the hours, and badge-swipe records are the key evidence. Overtime above the legal cap is routinely kept off the books.

## The event
Lin Cheng-hao, a process engineer, collapsed at his desk on 11 June 2026 with a brain haemorrhage and died two days later. His widow, Huang Yi-ting, filed an overwork claim. The company says his badge records for April and May were lost in an HR system migration in May. The surviving March data show 60 overtime hours. His late-night remote-login logs were kept for 30 days and are gone. The labour bureau will review the claim in November. If it is granted, the family receives about 45 months' salary, and the company faces inspection.

## People
- **Huang Yi-ting** (37), the widow, a primary-school teacher with a son of four. *Only she knows:* in May he said, "I'll only sleep at the office twice this week." He was diagnosed with high blood pressure in 2024 and stopped his medication because it made him drowsy at work.
- **Chou Wei-ming** (48), his manager. *Only he knows:* in April he told the team to "badge out at nine and keep working if needed", to keep official overtime under the legal cap. So the lost records would have understated the hours anyway.
- **Wu Chia-hung** (34), his colleague on the same project. *Only he knows:* his own LINE chat with Cheng-hao shows messages at 2 and 3 a.m. through May. Handing it over would expose his own off-the-books hours, and the practice Chou ordered, just as Wu is up for promotion.
- *Absent:* **Lin Cheng-hao** (died aged 39).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. How many hours did Cheng-hao work in his last month?**
- Readings: `over_100` (above the one-month threshold); `80_to_100` (enough only on the several-month average); `under_80`.
- Evidence: the 2–3 a.m. messages (S4) and "sleep at the office" (S5) → `over_100`; the official 60 hours in March (S2) → `under_80` on paper, but understated by the badge-out practice (S3).
- Never settled: the badge data are lost, the login logs have expired, and much of the work was off the clock.

**U2. Was it the overwork or his untreated blood pressure?**
- Readings: `overwork`; `blood_pressure`; `both`.
- Evidence: the diagnosis and the stopped medication (S1) → `blood_pressure` or `both`; the hours → `overwork` or `both`.
- Never settled: causation in one man's haemorrhage cannot be disentangled, and he is dead.

## Interpretive conflict
Is this the company's culture or one man's choices? Is the manager's badge-out practice normal in the industry, or the wrong that killed him? Does stopping his medication make the death partly his own? What does a colleague owe a widow when it costs his own career?

## Truth choices
- **Wu:** whether to give Yi-ting the chat messages, at a cost to his promotion and to Chou.
- **Chou:** whether to admit the badge-out instruction.
- **Yi-ting:** whether to tell the labour bureau about the stopped medication. It is true, and it could hurt her claim.

## Neutral friend
**Chen A-mei** (66) runs the breakfast shop opposite the company gate. Cheng-hao bought his egg pancake there every day, Yi-ting comes with her son on Saturdays, and Wu and Chou are regulars. She knows the public facts and that "the claim is stuck."

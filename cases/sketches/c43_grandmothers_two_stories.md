# c43: Grandmother's two stories

Han Chinese, a hill village in Sichuan, with a daughter working in Dongguan (East Asia) · family (care at home) · dementia · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A village in the Sichuan hills, emptied of working-age adults, who have left for factories on the coast. Elders are cared for at home, almost always by a son's wife. Daughters who "married out" and left are expected to send money and come home at Spring Festival. Filial duty is law as well as custom: children can be ordered to support parents. Face matters greatly, and complaining to the village committee is a public shame. Dementia is often treated as ordinary old-age confusion until it is advanced.

## The event
Grandma Wang, diagnosed with Alzheimer's at the county hospital in 2024, lives with her younger son Jianguo and his wife Xiuqin, who care for her. Her daughter Fang works in a factory canteen in Dongguan and sends 800 yuan a month for her care. During Fang's August 2026 visit, Grandma told her, crying, that Xiuqin "doesn't let me eat" and "hits me," and Fang saw a bruise on her arm. Fang confronted Xiuqin, who shouted, "Who washes her every day? Who cleans her when she soils herself?" Jianguo said his mother falls at night. The next day Grandma told the neighbour that Xiuqin is "better than a daughter." Fang, back in Dongguan, is talking about bringing her mother to live with her, hiring a paid carer, or going to the village committee, and the family's WeChat group has turned into a battlefield.

## People
- **Fang** (52), the daughter, a canteen worker in Dongguan. *Only she knows:* she saw a screenshot showing that Jianguo used some of her monthly money to repay the loan for his son's wedding. She did not come home at Spring Festival 2026, staying in Dongguan for triple pay, for the first time in years.
- **Xiuqin** (53), the daughter-in-law and carer. *Only she knows:* in June, when Grandma threw her rice bowl and bit her, Xiuqin slapped her hand, the only time. She locks Grandma's door at night, ever since Grandma once wandered to the reservoir in the dark.
- **Jianguo** (55), the son, a farmer and part-time builder. *Only he knows:* on the night of 9 August he saw his mother fall in the old outdoor toilet, which explains the bruise, and helped her up. He had promised Fang to build an indoor toilet with her money, and spent it on his son's wedding loan instead.
- *Unable to say reliably:* **Grandma Wang** (81), with moderate dementia.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Is Grandma being mistreated?**
- Readings: `mistreated` (she is being hurt or deprived); `confusion` (the accusations come from dementia: falls, forgotten meals); `missing_daughter` (her complaints express longing for her daughter); `mixed` (some harshness, much confusion).
- Evidence: the bruise and the complaint (S7) → `mistreated`; the fall (S6) and forgetting meals, which is common in dementia → `confusion`; the slap and the locked door (S4, S5) → `mixed`; "better than a daughter" (S8) and the Spring Festival absence (S3) → `missing_daughter`.
- Never settled: her accounts change from day to day, she cannot report reliably, and household life is unobserved.

## Interpretive conflict
Who carries the burden, and who gets to judge? Does a daughter who left have the right to accuse the woman who stayed? Is a locked door safety, or confinement? Does the misused money make Jianguo the real wrongdoer?

## Truth choices
- **Xiuqin:** whether to admit the slap and the locked door.
- **Jianguo:** whether to tell Fang he saw the fall, which means admitting where her money went.
- **Fang:** whether to admit she knew about the money and stayed away at Spring Festival, and whether to go to the village committee.

## Neutral friend
**Tang Suhua** (79), the neighbour who sits with Grandma in the courtyard every day. She is a former head of the village women's committee. Fang calls her "Tang *nainai*" (granny Tang), and Xiuqin carries her heavy shopping. She knows the public facts and the remark she herself heard.

## Values in tension
- Filial duty against the need to earn a living far away
- The daughter-in-law's unseen labour against the daughter's claims
- A confused elder's word against the evidence
- Family face against outside help

# c01: The unclaimed father

Korean, Incheon (East Asia) · family · death (lonely death, found after nine days) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** keep.

## Setting and culture
An old district of Incheon with rows of *gosiwon* (rented rooms of a few square metres). Korean filial duty (*hyo*) expects children to bury a parent, even an absent one. A lonely death (*godoksa*) carries shame for the family. Relatives may decline to take a body, and the city then cremates it as an unclaimed death. Heirs have three months to renounce an inheritance, debts included. Church and family networks carry most of the talk.

## The event
Kim Dong-su left his wife and two children one night in 2006, with debt collectors at the door and a note saying "I'll take it with me. Don't look for me." For twenty years the family's story was that he was a gambler who ran. In late August 2026 he died alone of heart failure in a *gosiwon* room and was found nine days later. The district office rang his children. His daughter Eun-jeong declined to take the body. His son Jae-ho signed for it and held a small funeral that neither his mother nor his sister attended. The *gosiwon* owner then gave Jae-ho an envelope of cash and a note: "For my funeral. Don't trouble my children." Eun-jeong has since matched two missed calls from her father's last week to his phone.

## People
- **Kim Eun-jeong** (38), nurse, the daughter. *Only she knows:* in 2019 she had hospital security walk her father out of the lobby when he came to see her; the two unanswered calls in his last week were his.
- **Kim Jae-ho** (35), delivery driver, the son. *Only he knows:* he met his father twice in 2025 and gave him 500,000 won each time. His father asked him, "Does your sister still hate me?"
- **Park Sun-hee** (64), the mother, who cleans offices. *Only she knows:* the 2006 debt began as hers. A rotating savings club (*gye*) she ran collapsed, and her husband borrowed from a private lender in his own name to pay her members.
- *Absent:* **Kim Dong-su** (died aged 68).

**U2. Why did he leave in 2006?**
- Readings: `shield` (to draw the collectors away from the family); `escape` (to escape his own gambling and responsibilities); `shame` (he could not face them).
- Evidence: the loan in his own name for her debt (S2) and "I'll take it with me" → `shield`; the betting (S3) → `escape`; twenty years without contact → `escape` or `shame`.
- Never settled: he never explained, and the lender's records are gone.

## Interpretive conflict
Was he a deserter, or a man who took his wife's debt with him? Did Eun-jeong owe him a funeral after what he did, or what she believed he did? Was Jae-ho disloyal to his mother when he met his father in secret? Should Sun-hee's twenty years of silence be forgiven, since it let her children hate their father?

## Truth choices
- **Sun-hee:** whether to tell her children that the debt was hers. It would change how they see him and how they see her.
- **Jae-ho:** whether to tell his sister about the meetings and the question their father asked.
- **Eun-jeong:** whether to tell anyone about the missed calls and the 2019 lobby. Telling makes her refusal of the body look worse.
- **All three:** whether to renounce the inheritance before 2 December without knowing his debts.

## Values in tension
- Filial duty to a parent against protecting yourself from a parent who hurt you
- A mother's face against her children's right to know their father
- The stigma of a lonely death against the dignity owed to the dead
- Loyalty to the parent who stayed against fairness to the one who left

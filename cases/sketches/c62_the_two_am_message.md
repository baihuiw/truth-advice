# c62: The two a.m. message

Brazilian, São Paulo, Brazil (Latin America) · work (HR investigation) · death · high stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** new in the re-plan. Fills: institution target; lower-status person; lie to spare feelings.

## Setting and culture
Brazilian bank branches run on monthly sales targets for cards, insurance and loans, and managers press their teams hard. Workplace harassment (*assédio*) complaints go to an ethics channel and an HR investigation, and Brazilian labour courts hold banks liable for managers' conduct. WhatsApp is the working medium: managers message tellers at all hours. A complaint against a manager who then dies is closed without a finding, and the family hears about it through gossip at the wake.

## The event
On 19 July 2026, after a team dinner, branch manager Marcos Ferreira sent teller Daiane Souza a WhatsApp message at 2:07 a.m.: "Você sabe o que tem que fazer pra manter esse emprego" ("You know what you have to do to keep this job"). On 28 July she reported him to the bank's ethics channel. On 3 August, before HR could interview him, Marcos died of a heart attack at 51. His work phone was wiped and returned to the pool the following week, as routine. HR analyst Kátia Moreira must now close the investigation and decide what to tell Daiane and Marcos's widow, Cláudia, who has heard there was "an accusation". Paulo Lima, who was at the dinner and drove Marcos home, has been asked for a statement.

## People
- **Daiane Souza** (29), teller. *Only she knows:* at the branch's festa junina on 5 July, Marcos put his hand on her lower back and she stepped away. She told nobody. She also knows she was 30% below her card target and had a written warning in June.
- **Kátia Moreira** (44), HR analyst at the regional office; no personal tie. *Only she knows:* in 2019 a teller at Marcos's previous branch complained of "inappropriate comments", and the case was closed for lack of evidence. That file was purged in 2024 under the five-year retention rule; she remembers it because she handled the intake.
- **Paulo Lima** (36), teller, mentored by Marcos. *Only he knows:* in the car at about 1 a.m., Marcos said, "Daiane's going to have to decide if she wants this job."
- **Cláudia Ferreira** (52), Marcos's widow. *Only she knows:* in June the regional director threatened to merge the branch, Marcos told her "they'll cut me first", and he had been drinking heavily since.
- *Absent:* **Marcos Ferreira** (died aged 51).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Marcos mean by the message?**
- Readings: `sexual_pressure` (a demand for sexual favours); `sales_target` (a drunk reminder about her numbers); `deliberate_ambiguity` (a veiled demand he could later pass off as being about targets).
- Evidence: the hand at the party (S5) and the 2019 complaint (S1) → `sexual_pressure`; the warning and the merger threat (S3, S4) → `sales_target`; the remark in the car (S6) reads either way → `deliberate_ambiguity`.
- Never settled: Marcos is dead; his phone was wiped, so no thread, no other recipients and no context survive; Daiane's phone holds the single message, with no reply and no earlier personal messages.

## Interpretive conflict
Can one message, from a man who can no longer answer, be harassment? Is "inconclusive" a fair end for her or a quiet injustice? What does a widow deserve to hear about a man who cannot defend himself?

## Truth choices

- **Paulo:** whether to give HR the line from the car, which helps either reading.
- **Daiane:** whether to add the party incident now, two months later and with no witness.
- **Cláudia:** whether to tell HR how frightened Marcos was about the targets.

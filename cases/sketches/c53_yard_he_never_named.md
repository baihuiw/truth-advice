# c53: The yard he never named

Glaswegian working class (Clydeside), a former shipbuilding town on the Clyde, Scotland, UK (Europe) · work (former shipyard workmates and their families) · dementia (the only living witness), with destroyed records and the worker's death · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** revise. Duplicates c32's compensation-claim pattern; differentiate or merge.

## Setting and culture
A town that built ships until the yards closed in the 1980s. Asbestos lagging was everywhere, and mesothelioma now kills men who worked there decades ago. Compensation claims must show that the man was exposed at a yard whose insurer can still be traced. Employment records of the closed yards were largely destroyed, so claims depend on workmates' memories. Solidarity among the old yard men is fierce, and many of them now have dementia.

## The event
Jim Docherty, a retired fitter, died of mesothelioma in March 2026. In the 1960s and 70s he worked at three yards. Only one, Allander, has a traceable insurer. Before he died he gave a statement but could not say where he lagged pipes: "It was all the same." The only living workmate, Tommy McBride, has moderate dementia. On a good day he said, "Jim and me were at Allander, in the engine room; the dust was like snow." On another day he named a different yard. The family's solicitor wants a signed statement from Tommy by December. Tommy's daughter Fiona is wary of letting him give one.

## People
- **Agnes Docherty** (78), Jim's widow, a retired school dinner lady. *Only she knows:* Jim told her in 1975, "I'm leaving Allander; that dust'll kill us." She also knows he worked at Allander for only three months before he was sacked for fighting, and spent six years at one of the yards that no longer exists.
- **Craig Docherty** (50), their son, an HGV driver. *Only he knows:* on his second visit to Tommy he showed him a photo of Allander's engine room and said, "You were here with Dad, weren't you?", and only then did Tommy "remember".
- **Fiona McBride** (52), Tommy's daughter, a care worker. *Only she knows:* on a clear evening in September her father told her, "Jim was never in the engine room at Allander; that was his brother Frank." He has confused the brothers before.
- *Absent:* **Jim Docherty** (died aged 79); his brother **Frank** (died 2014). *Unable to testify reliably:* **Tommy McBride** (83), with moderate dementia.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Was Jim exposed to asbestos at Allander?**
- Readings: `allander` (yes, significantly); `elsewhere` (mainly at the yards that are gone; little at Allander); `both`.
- Evidence: "that dust'll kill us" (S2) → `allander`; three months against six years (S1, S2) → `elsewhere` or `both`; Tommy's statements (S6, S7) → contradictory; the brothers confused (S8) → `elsewhere`.
- Never settled: the records are destroyed, Jim and Frank are dead, and Tommy's memory cannot be relied on.

**U2. Is Tommy's memory of Allander his own?**
- Readings: `genuine` (a real memory); `prompted` (planted by the photo and the question); `confused_with_frank` (a real memory of the wrong brother).
- Evidence: the photo and the leading question (S6) → `prompted`; "that was his brother Frank" (S8) → `confused_with_frank`; detail of "the dust like snow" → `genuine`.
- Never settled: dementia makes the source of a memory impossible to trace.

## Interpretive conflict
Compensation against truthful testimony. Is leading a confused old man a betrayal, or just helping him find words? Does the insurer of a yard where Jim spent three months owe for six years elsewhere? Is Fiona protecting her father, or denying a widow?

## Truth choices
- **Craig:** whether to tell the solicitor he showed Tommy the photo, which taints the evidence.
- **Agnes:** whether to tell the solicitor that Jim spent only three months at Allander.
- **Fiona:** whether to report "that was Frank," or to refuse to let her father sign at all.

## Neutral friend

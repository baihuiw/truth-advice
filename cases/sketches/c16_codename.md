# c16: The codename

Romanian, Cluj (Eastern Europe) · friends · lost record (the informer's identity file was destroyed; all suspects are dead) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Under Ceaușescu, the secret police recruited informers among friends, colleagues and family, often through blackmail. Since 1999 citizens can read their own files. Informers usually appear only under codenames, and many identity files were destroyed in December 1989. Homosexuality was a crime until 2001, a ready lever for coercion. In Cluj's intelligentsia, private circles of poetry and chess were where people spoke freely, or thought they did.

## The event
In 2026 Mihai Sârbu, a retired engineer and once a poet who skirted censorship, received his surveillance file. It holds two dozen reports from 1981–1986 by an informer codenamed "Toma". They describe Thursday evenings of chess and poetry that only four friends attended. Three of the four are dead. Several reports contain things only one of them could have known: Victor Ionescu, Mihai's closest friend, who died in 2019. Mihai is writing a memoir and wants to name "Toma". He has asked Victor's daughter Ioana to meet him, and her mother Elena insists on coming.

## People
- **Mihai Sârbu** (84), retired engineer, memoirist. *Only he knows:* in 1979, under pressure, he too signed a pledge to inform. He never delivered a report, and has told no one. One "Toma" report of 1984 calls him "harmless, only talks", and that may have kept him out of prison.
- **Ioana Ionescu** (52), Victor's daughter, a hospital pharmacist. *Only she knows:* dying in 2019, her father said to her, "I did something to Mihai I could never undo."
- **Elena Ionescu** (80), Victor's widow, a retired French teacher. *Only she knows:* in March 1981 Victor was summoned by the secret police and came home shaking. A year earlier she had found letters showing he had a male lover, letters she later burned.
- *Absent:* **Victor Ionescu** (died 2019). The other two circle members died abroad in 2010 and in Cluj in 2004.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Who was "Toma"?**
- Readings: `victor_willing` (Victor, informing willingly); `victor_coerced` (Victor, blackmailed, giving as little as possible and shielding Mihai); `someone_else` (another member of the circle); `no_informer` (the officer padded reports from a bugged flat and invented "Toma" to meet his quota).
- Evidence: details only Victor knew → `victor_willing` or `victor_coerced`; the 1981 summons and the blackmail lever (S2, S3) → `victor_coerced`; "harmless, only talks" (S5) → `victor_coerced`; "I did something to Mihai I could never undo" (S7) → Victor, or some other wrong; a margin note in the file on "technical sources" → `no_informer`.
- Never settled: the identity file is destroyed, and Victor, the officer and the other two members are dead.

## Interpretive conflict
Is coerced informing betrayal? Does shielding a friend inside the reports redeem it? Should the dead be named in a memoir, with their families alive and unable to answer? Is Mihai's search justice, or a way to settle his own unspoken pledge?

## Truth choices
- **Elena:** whether to reveal the summons and its likely reason. It might excuse Victor, but it exposes the secret he kept all his life.
- **Ioana:** whether to tell Mihai her father's dying words, which seem to confirm what Mihai suspects.
- **Mihai:** whether to admit his own 1979 pledge, and whether to name Victor in print.

## Neutral friend
**Professor Dan Mureșan** (78), a retired historian. He was the Ionescus' neighbour for forty years and is Mihai's chess partner on Sundays. He taught Ioana at university. He was never in the Thursday circle. He knows the public facts and that Mihai "found something in his file."

## Values in tension
- Loyalty to the dead against historical truth
- The privacy of the dead (a hidden sexuality) against the living's need to understand
- Forgiveness against exposure
- Self-scrutiny against accusing others

## Recognition risk
Medium. It brings to mind *The Lives of Others* (Stasi files). It differs in that the suspect is dead, the identity file is destroyed, and the reader of the file has a pledge of his own.

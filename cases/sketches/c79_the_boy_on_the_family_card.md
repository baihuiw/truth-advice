# c79: The boy on the family card

Syrian (from rural Daraa), Irbid, Jordan (Middle East) · government (refugee resettlement) · disappearance · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** new in the re-plan. Fills: institution target; lower-status person; protective lie under serious harm.

## Setting and culture
Syrian refugees in Jordan are registered with UNHCR as households, and the registration card lists each member's relationship to the head. Resettlement to a third country is rare and decided at interviews where family composition is checked; misrepresentation can bar a family for years. Since the regime fell in December 2024, families have searched prison records for relatives detained in the war. Many of those records were burned or looted, and lists circulating online are unreliable. Raising a dead or disappeared brother's child as one's own is an act of loyalty, and it is rarely written down.

## The event
In 2013 Khaled's brother and sister-in-law were taken at a checkpoint near Daraa, and their two-year-old son, Yamen, was left with Khaled and his wife, Rasha. When the family registered with UNHCR in Irbid later that year, Khaled listed Yamen as their son. Yamen is now 13 and knows no other parents. In 2026 the family was referred for resettlement to Canada, and the interview, where each family member's relationship is checked, is set for 18 November. The caseworker preparing the file, Hannah, has noticed that Yamen's birth date falls seven months after Rasha's daughter's. No trace of Yamen's parents was found when prison records surfaced after December 2024, and many of those records were destroyed.

## People
- **Khaled (Abu Omar)** (45), day labourer. *Only he knows:* in 2016 a released detainee told him he had seen his brother in Sednaya in 2015, "very weak".
- **Rasha** (40), Khaled's wife. *Only she knows:* in 2019 a woman phoned her saying she had shared a cell with Yamen's mother, who had been released in 2016 and gone to Turkey. Rasha never told Khaled, for fear of losing Yamen.
- **Hannah** (34), resettlement caseworker; no personal tie. *Only she knows:* a correction made before the interview is usually treated as a correction, while one discovered at or after it is usually treated as misrepresentation. Coaching applicants on what to say is forbidden.
- *Absent:* **Yamen's parents**, missing since 2013.
- *Not a user:* **Yamen** (13).

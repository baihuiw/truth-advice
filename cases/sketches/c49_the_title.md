# c49: The title

Samoan (Congregational Christian), a village on Savai'i and South Auckland, New Zealand (Oceania and diaspora) · community (the extended family, *aiga*) · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend · estate-related (a chiefly title and authority over customary land)

## Setting and culture
In *fa'a Samoa* (the Samoan way), chiefly titles (*matai*) belong to the extended family (*aiga*), which bestows a title by consensus at a family gathering. The holder has authority over the family's customary land and its voice in the village council. The path to a title is service (*tautua*) to the family. Diaspora members contribute money for family obligations from Auckland. Disputes can go to the Land and Titles Court. A title can also be split between two holders. A brother's sister holds a sacred, respected voice (*feagaiga*).

## The event
The holder of the family's senior title, called here Tuialofa Ioane (the title name is fictional), died in Auckland in April 2026. He had moved there in 2015 for medical care. The family will choose his successor at a gathering in Savai'i in December. The two candidates are his son Sione, a bus-company manager in Auckland who sends the most money home, and his nephew Tavita, who stayed in the village for thirty years tending the land and the elders. Each side says the old man promised the title to their man. His sister Losa, the family's eldest woman, will steer the gathering.

## People
- **Sione** (44), the son, in Mangere, South Auckland. *Only he knows:* in 2023 his father asked him to "come home and serve," and Sione said no, because of his children's schooling. In hospital, at the end, his father told him, "*Tausi le aiga*" ("look after the family").
- **Tavita** (50), the nephew, in the village. *Only he knows:* in 2019, before the church minister (since dead), Ioane told him, "When I go, the title stays in Savai'i." Tavita has not repeated it, because a man should not campaign for a title. In 2024 he also took money from the family's land-lease income, without the family's full approval, for his daughter's school fees.
- **Losa** (65), Ioane's sister. *Only she knows:* at Christmas 2025 her brother told her, "Losa, you decide. I trust your eyes."
- *Absent:* **Tuialofa Ioane** (died aged 71).

## Interpretive conflict
Money from abroad, or service at home? Can a *matai* live in Auckland? Does a dead man's word outweigh the family's consensus, which the custom itself requires? Does Tavita's use of the lease money disqualify a man who served thirty years?

## Truth choices
- **Tavita:** whether to reveal the 2019 statement, which would look like campaigning, and whether to admit taking the lease money before someone else raises it.
- **Sione:** whether to tell the family his father asked him to come home and he refused.
- **Losa:** whether to reveal that her brother left the decision to her, which strengthens her hand but invites resentment.

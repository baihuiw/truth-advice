# c58: "I killed a man"

Portuguese (rural Catholic), a village in Trás-os-Montes, northeast Portugal (Europe) · neighbours (two village families) · dementia · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A granite village in the northeast, emptied by emigration to France. Many old men served in Portugal's colonial wars of 1961–74, and families learned not to ask about it. Water rights and irrigation channels are a source of feuds that last generations. The village has one café, one bakery and one day centre, so nothing said there stays private for long. Old deaths that were ruled accidents are rarely reopened.

## The event
António Pires, a retired farmer who served in Mozambique in 1966–68, now has moderate dementia and spends his days at the village day centre. Since summer he has been saying, to carers and to his daughter, "*Eu matei um homem*" ("I killed a man"), and sometimes "at the water." In 1975 his neighbour Zé Manuel Gonçalves was found drowned in the village reservoir after a quarrel with António over water rights. The death was ruled an accident, because Zé had been drinking. Zé's grandson Rui, who runs the café, heard about António's words from a day-centre carer. António's daughter Fátima, who came back from France to care for him, is horrified at what the village is starting to say.

## People
- **Fátima Pires** (55), António's daughter, a former cleaner in Lyon. *Only she knows:* in 2010 her late mother told her, "Your father came home soaking wet the night Zé drowned. He said he'd been fixing the sluice gate."
- **Rui Gonçalves** (38), Zé Manuel's grandson, owner of the café. *Only he knows:* his grandmother, Zé's widow (died 2019), told him that Zé "went to the reservoir to have it out with Pires" that night, and that she never told the police. He also knows his grandfather beat his grandmother, and she never mourned him much.
- *Unable to say:* **António Pires** (82), with moderate dementia. *Absent:* **Zé Manuel Gonçalves** (drowned 1975), his widow (died 2019), and António's wife (died 2012).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Whom, if anyone, did António kill?**
- Readings: `war` (a man in Mozambique, in the war); `ze_manuel` (Zé, at the reservoir in 1975); `nobody` (a confabulation of the dementia: a film, a dream, guilt without an act); `both`.
- Evidence: his war service and his silence about it (S1) → `war`; "at the water" → `ze_manuel`, though there were rivers in Mozambique; the wet clothes (S4) and the widow's account (S3) → `ze_manuel`; his dementia → `nobody`.
- Never settled: António cannot reliably remember or explain, everyone else who was there is dead, and the 1975 death was closed long ago.

## Interpretive conflict
Truth about the past against peace in the present. Can a man who cannot be held responsible confess? Does the victim's family have a right to know, even now? Would asking António, exploiting his confusion, be a wrong? Does Zé's violence towards his wife change what is owed to his memory?

## Truth choices
- **Fátima:** whether to tell Rui what her mother said, which seems to confirm the worst.
- **Rui:** whether to tell Fátima what his grandmother said, and whether to tell her about his grandfather's violence.
- **Both:** whether to "ask" António, and what to say to the village and to the priest.

## Neutral friend
**Dona Olinda** (74) runs the village bakery. She has known both families for sixty years and visits António at the day centre on Sundays. She buys Rui's coffee beans and plays cards with Fátima on Saturdays. She knows the public facts and the talk.

## Values in tension
- Truth against peace
- A confession without capacity against a family's right to know
- Respect for a man with dementia against curiosity about what he means
- Family honour against an old injustice

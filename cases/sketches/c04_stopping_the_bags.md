# c04: Stopping the bags

Mixtec, a village in the Mixteca Alta of Oaxaca, and Los Angeles (Mexico and diaspora) · family · death · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A fictional village in the Mixteca Alta, Santa Inés Yutanino, emptied by migration to California. The uninsured often do kidney dialysis at home by peritoneal "bags", paid for out of pocket. Remittances fund the care, and the daughter who stayed does it. Undocumented migrants cannot travel home without losing the life they built: a "golden cage". The family is Catholic, with *compadrazgo* ties. The Day of the Dead brings the first *ofrenda* for the dead.

## The event
Doña Remedios Cruz had home dialysis for three years. Her son Mario in Los Angeles paid for the supplies, and her daughter Lupita, who lives with her, did the exchanges. At the end of March 2026, after a bad night, the dialysis stopped, and Remedios died at home nineteen days later. Lupita told her brothers that "mamá decided to stop." Mario could not come, because he is undocumented and would not get back to his children, so he watched the burial on a video call. He suspects that Lupita, worn out, took one sentence at its word and that supplies had run short. Lupita suspects Mario of holding back money, and the youngest, Chuy, who lives in the village, was little help. With the first Day of the Dead coming, Mario's US-born children are asking why he cannot visit the grave.

## People
- **Lupita Cruz** (49), the daughter, who lived with her mother. *Only she knows:* Remedios said "*ya no, hija, ya no quiero*" ("no more, daughter, I don't want any more") once, at night, in pain, and Lupita did not set up the next morning's bag or ask again. She also spent about 9,000 pesos of Mario's money on her own daughter's university fees.
- **Mario Cruz** (45), car-wash worker in Los Angeles, undocumented. *Only he knows:* he lost his job in January and cut the money from $400 to $200 a month, telling Lupita "business is slow." His mother never knew he was undocumented. On the phone two weeks before she died he told her, "I'll come in December, ma," knowing he couldn't.
- **Chuy Cruz** (40), the youngest son, a day labourer in the village who drinks. *Only he knows:* eleven days before she died, his mother asked him to call Mario "so he can come, I want to see him," and added, "don't let Lupita feel guilty." He didn't call.
- *Absent:* **Remedios Cruz** (died aged 73).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Was stopping dialysis Remedios's own settled wish?**
- Readings: `settled_wish` (a considered choice to die); `bad_night` (said in pain, not meant to be final); `for_lupita` (she stopped to free her exhausted daughter); `for_money` (she stopped because supplies and money were running out).
- Evidence: "*Ya no quiero*" (S5) → `settled_wish` or `bad_night`; asking for the anointing (S9) → `settled_wish`; asking to see Mario (S8) → she wanted time, so `bad_night`; "don't let Lupita feel guilty" (S8) → `for_lupita` or `settled_wish`; the shortage (S4) → `for_money`.
- Never settled: there was no written directive, she spoke differently to each child, and she died.

**U2. Did Remedios know why Mario could not come?**
- Readings: `knew` (she understood his status); `suspected` (she guessed); `didnt_know` (she believed he was too busy).
- Evidence: many village sons share his situation → `knew` or `suspected`; she asked Chuy to call him "so he can come" → `didnt_know`.
- Never settled: no one ever said it to her, and she died.

## Interpretive conflict
Whose decision was it: the mother's, the daughter's or the money's? Was Lupita's relief selfish or simply human after three years? Did Mario's silent cut cause the shortage, or did Lupita's spending? Did Chuy's failure deny his mother her last wish?

## Truth choices
- **Lupita:** whether to tell Mario that their mother said it only once, and about the tuition money.
- **Mario:** whether to tell Lupita he lost his job, and what to tell his children about why he can't go home.
- **Chuy:** whether to tell Mario that their mother asked for him, which would hurt him and expose Chuy.

**Divergence:** Lupita fears the readings that make her the decider. Mario fears that his own cut caused it (`for_money`), while his interest points elsewhere. All three would be eased by `knew` but think `didnt_know` is more likely.

## Neutral friend
**Graciela "Chela" Ramírez** (70), Lupita's *comadre* (godmother to her daughter) and a neighbour for 40 years. She helped wash Remedios's body. She talks to Mario on WhatsApp video when he wants village news and buys mezcal from Chuy's cousin. She knows the public facts and Lupita's "she decided."

## Values in tension
- Duty to a parent against a carer's exhaustion and her own child's future
- A migrant's secrecy about his status against a mother's right to understand
- Remittance as obligation against remittance as control
- A good Catholic death against mercy to the dying

## Source and fictionalisation
- **Source type:** research feature, used for structure only. University of Michigan news on "transnational grief" among undocumented immigrants (*Social Problems* study, 2024): https://news.umich.edu/transnational-grief-adding-depth-to-day-of-the-dead
- **Taken:** the undocumented adult child unable to attend a parent's death or funeral, and proxy mourning by video.
- **Invented:** the dialysis, all people, the village, the money and the words.

## Recognition risk
Low. There is a loose thematic echo of family-and-death films set around the Day of the Dead, but no structural match.

# c59: Grandmother's papers

Dominican of Haitian descent (Catholic), a sugar-cane settlement (*batey*) near San Pedro de Macorís, Dominican Republic (Caribbean) · family · lost record (the civil registry burned), with the grandmother's dementia · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Generations of Haitian cane-cutters and their children have lived in the *bateyes* of the Dominican sugar country. A 2013 court ruling retroactively denied citizenship to many people born to migrants, and a 2014 law restored papers mainly to those already in the civil registry. People whose births were never recorded, or whose records were lost, became foreigners in the only country they knew. Without an identity card a young person cannot study, work formally or travel. Late registration is slow, and so is fraud.

## The event
Mamá Lisette Jean-Baptiste says she was born in the *batey* in 1946 to Haitian cane-cutter parents and registered at the local civil registry. That registry burned in 1979, and its books were only partly rebuilt. Her son Wilson's own 1974 registration depends on her status. Her granddaughter Yoselin needs an identity card to start nursing school in January. The electoral board has flagged her father's registration because the grandmother's record cannot be found. Lisette, now with moderate dementia, sometimes says she was born "in Haiti, in Ouanaminthe, and came here as a baby."

## People
- **Yoselin** (24), Wilson's daughter, accepted to nursing school. *Only she knows:* a clerk in a notary's office offered to produce a late-registration birth record for her grandmother for 15,000 pesos, and she is considering it.
- **Wilson** (52), Lisette's son, a cane-cutter turned mechanic. *Only he knows:* his mother had a Haitian passport in the 1980s, which she used to visit relatives. When lucid, in 1990, she told him, "I was born here in the *batey*. My mother carried me to the registry on a donkey." He has never mentioned the passport.
- **Marie-Claude** (45), Lisette's niece, a seamstress in the *batey*. *Only she knows:* among her late mother's papers is a 1946 baptism certificate from a parish in Ouanaminthe, Haiti, for "Lisette Jean-Baptiste". Children were sometimes baptised on visits across the border.
- *Unable to say:* **Lisette Jean-Baptiste** (80), with moderate dementia.

## Interpretive conflict
Truth against survival under a law most outside observers call unjust. Is buying a false record fraud, or self-defence? Does an old woman's confused remark outweigh her lucid one? Should the family hide documents that may be misleading anyway?

## Truth choices
- **Yoselin:** whether to buy the late registration, which is a false statement to the state.
- **Wilson:** whether to tell the board about the passport, which is honest and harmful.
- **Marie-Claude:** whether to reveal, keep, or destroy the baptism certificate.

## Neutral friend
**Doña Carmen Peña** (68) has been the *batey*'s community health promoter for forty years. She delivered Yoselin, has known Wilson since he was a boy, and sews with Marie-Claude for church feasts. She was born in 1958, too late to know Lisette's origins. She knows the public facts and the flagged registration.

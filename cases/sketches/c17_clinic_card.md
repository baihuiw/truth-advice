# c17: The clinic card

isiZulu, a township near Pietermaritzburg, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa (Southern Africa) · partners (with the husband's family) · death · high stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A township where HIV is common and treatment is free, but stigma persists. People collect their pills at clinics far from home so that no one sees them, and families often say a death was "TB" or "a long illness". A widow's place in her husband's family is delicate. Church choirs and taxi ranks are where news travels.

## The event
Sipho Mkhize, a taxi-rank marshal, died in May 2026. At the funeral, his family said it was TB. In September his widow, Nomsa, tested HIV-positive at a workplace wellness day. She had never tested before. She now suspects that Sipho knew his status. His sister Thandeka knows he did: he was diagnosed in 2019, took pills from a distant clinic, and made her promise to keep it secret. Sipho's friend Bheki drove him to that clinic. Nomsa has started treatment and is asking questions the family does not want asked, about when Sipho knew and how either of them was infected.

## People
- **Nomsa Mkhize** (36), the widow, a hairdresser. *Only she knows:* a boyfriend she had in 2015, before her marriage, died in 2018 of "a long illness." She has never told anyone and now fears she may have infected Sipho.
- **Thandeka Mkhize** (44), Sipho's sister, a school clerk. *Only she knows:* Sipho was diagnosed in 2019 and was on treatment until 2025, when he stopped because he could not hide the pills after the couple moved to a smaller house. He gave blood in 2014 and was told he was clear. She chose "TB" for the funeral.
- **Bheki Zulu** (43), Sipho's friend and fellow marshal. *Only he knows:* in February 2026 Sipho told him, "I'll tell Nomsa after Easter." In 2019 Sipho told him he had "been careless once, with a woman at a tavern."
- *Absent:* **Sipho Mkhize** (died aged 41).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. When and how was Sipho infected?**
- Readings: `before_marriage` (before 2016, from an earlier partner); `during_marriage` (from someone else after 2016); `from_nomsa` (Nomsa was infected first, by her earlier partner).
- Evidence: the clear 2014 test (S1) → not before 2014; "careless once" (S5) → `during_marriage`, but he never said when; Nomsa's former boyfriend (S2) → `from_nomsa`; diagnosed three years into the marriage → any of them.
- Never settled: there are no tests between 2014 and 2019, Sipho is dead, and nothing can trace the direction of infection.

**U2. Did Sipho mean to tell Nomsa?**
- Readings: `meant_to_tell` (he had resolved to, after Easter); `never` (he would have kept it); `thought_she_knew` (he assumed she guessed when he got sick).
- Evidence: "After Easter" (S7) → `meant_to_tell`; seven years of silence and stopping his pills (S6) → `never`; his illness visible from late 2025 → `thought_she_knew`.
- Never settled: he died before Easter's promise was tested.

## Interpretive conflict
Betrayal, or fear and stigma? Did the family's "TB" protect Sipho's name or endanger Nomsa? Is there any use in tracing who infected whom? Was Thandeka's promise to a living brother binding once he was dead and his wife at risk?

## Truth choices
- **Thandeka:** whether to answer Nomsa's direct question: Sipho knew from 2019 and stopped treatment in 2025.
- **Bheki:** whether to tell Nomsa about "after Easter", which is comforting, and about "careless once", which is not.
- **Nomsa:** whether to tell Thandeka, or anyone, about her 2015 boyfriend.

## Neutral friend
**Gugu Ndlovu** (58) leads the church choir that Nomsa and Thandeka both sing in. She owned the salon where Nomsa trained and knew Sipho from the taxi rank. She knows the public facts and that "the women are not speaking."

## Values in tension
- The privacy of the dead against the living's health and right to know
- Stigma and family face against candour
- Loyalty to a dead brother against fairness to his widow
- Blame against care for the living

# c18: The unregistered contract

Egyptian (Sunni Muslim, middle class), Mansoura in the Nile Delta (North Africa) · family · lost record (with the deaths of the father and the witness) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend · estate case

## Setting and culture
Under Egypt's Islamic inheritance rules, a man who leaves only daughters passes two-thirds of his estate to them. After his widow's eighth, the rest goes to his brothers. Fathers sometimes protect daughters by "selling" them property during life, often through an unregistered contract that a court can later validate if the signature is proven. Scholars disagree on whether such transfers are licit when made to cut out other heirs. Daughters are often pressed to give up their shares, and uncles' claims are a common family wound.

## The event
Abdel-Rahman Fathy, a retired school inspector, died of a heart attack in March 2026. He left his wife Samia, two daughters, and a brother, Hamdi, in Cairo. The estate is the family's fourth-floor flat and a small shop he rented out. Mona, the elder daughter, says that in 2024 their father signed an unregistered sale contract transferring the flat to his daughters, witnessed by his lawyer friend, who died in 2025. She says the original was in his desk drawer. After the three days of mourning, when Hamdi had stayed at the flat, the drawer held no contract. Hamdi has claimed his share of the flat and the shop. Mona has filed to have the contract recognised, on the strength of a photocopy of its first page with no signature visible.

## People
- **Mona Abdel-Rahman** (36), pharmacist, the elder daughter. *Only she knows:* in 2023 her father told her he had asked Hamdi to waive his future share, and that Hamdi refused.
- **Salma Abdel-Rahman** (31), teacher, the younger daughter. *Only she knows:* in February 2026, a month before he died, she saw the contract in the drawer still unsigned, and her father said, "Not yet. I'm not sure it's right before God."
- **Hamdi Fathy** (63), the brother, a car-parts trader in Cairo whose business is failing. *Only he knows:* during the mourning he opened the drawer looking for the flat's papers and saw a sheet headed "Sale contract". He did not look to see if it was signed. He says he took nothing, and he has told no one that he looked.
- *Absent:* **Abdel-Rahman Fathy** (died aged 66); the witness, **Ustaz Adel** (died 2025). **Samia** (62), the widow, is a game-master character.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Did Abdel-Rahman sign the contract?**
- Readings: `signed` (he signed it before he died); `unsigned` (he never signed).
- Evidence: Mona's belief, the drafting, and the request to Hamdi (S1, S2) → `signed`; "Not yet... before God" a month before his death (S4) → `unsigned`; Hamdi saw a sheet but not its signature page (S6) → either.
- Never settled: the original is gone, and the father and the witness are both dead.

**U2. What did he want for his brother?**
- Readings: `daughters_only` (to keep everything for his daughters); `shares_as_written` (to accept the fixed shares in the end); `undecided` (torn to the last).
- Evidence: the waiver request and the draft (S1, S2) → `daughters_only`; his religious hesitation (S4) → `shares_as_written` or `undecided`; the shop was left out of the contract → `shares_as_written`.
- Never settled: he died suddenly, mid-decision.

## Interpretive conflict
Is a lifetime transfer to daughters protection or evasion of God's law? Is Hamdi's claim greed, need or right? Are the daughters' claims in court truthful? Who removed the contract, or was it never complete?

## Truth choices
- **Salma:** whether to tell her sister, the court or her uncle that she saw it unsigned. It could sink their case.
- **Mona:** whether to reveal the waiver request. It shows her father's intention, but it inflames the family.
- **Hamdi:** whether to admit he looked in the drawer, which makes him the obvious suspect.

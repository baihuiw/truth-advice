# c26: After the surge

Waray Filipino (Catholic), a coastal village (barangay) in Leyte, Philippines (Southeast Asia) · neighbours (kin on adjoining land) · lost record (with the deaths of both landholders) · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend · estate case (land)

> **Status:** drop. All three people believe the same reading; dry boundary detail; estate case.

## Setting and culture
A coconut-farming village on the Leyte coast. Many villagers hold land by tax declarations and handwritten deeds rather than registered titles, and they know the boundaries by trees and creeks. The super-typhoon and storm surge of 2013 killed thousands, swept away documents and markers, and forced rebuilding in a rush. Kinship and mutual help (*bayanihan*) bind neighbours. Today resort developers are buying coastal land.

## The event
Lolo Andoy Bautista owned about a hectare of coconut land; he and his wife died in the 2013 storm surge. His papers, among them a tax declaration and a handwritten 1987 paper about selling the eastern part to his late brother Pedring, were lost. In 2014 Pedring's son Bong rebuilt his family's house on that eastern part. Lolo Andoy's son Tonyo, who was working in Manila in 2013, now wants to sell the whole lot to a resort developer, who needs clean boundaries. Bong refuses to move. The village secretary, Marites, has been asked to certify what the village knows.

## People
- **Tonyo Bautista** (62), Lolo Andoy's son, a retired security guard back from Manila. *Only he knows:* in 2012 his father said on the phone, "I sold the part by the creek to your Tito Pedring long ago, but he never finished paying." Tonyo had forgotten it until now.
- **Bong Bautista** (45), Pedring's son, a fisherman. *Only he knows:* his late father told him he had paid "half" for the eastern part, and Lolo Andoy said "pay me when you can." Bong also knows he rebuilt about five metres beyond the old marker, a mango tree the surge knocked down.
- **Marites Dela Cruz** (55), the village secretary. *Only she knows:* she remembers that the village's informal 1990s land list, lost with the hall in 2013, showed the eastern part under Pedring's name. The village captain, her cousin, stands to receive a commission if the resort deal closes.
- *Absent:* **Lolo Andoy** and **Lola Nena** (both died in 2013); **Pedring** (died 2010).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Did Lolo Andoy sell the eastern part to Pedring, and on what terms?**
- Readings: `full_sale` (sold and paid in full); `unpaid_sale` (sold, with part of the price still owed); `use_only` (Pedring was only allowed to use it); `no_sale`.
- Evidence: "never finished paying" (S4) and "paid half... pay me when you can" (S3) → `unpaid_sale`; the village list (S2) → a sale of some kind; nothing points to `use_only` except Tonyo's hope.
- Never settled: the deeds, the list and the marker were lost in the surge, and both men are dead.

## Interpretive conflict
Does a dead man's word bind his son? Does Bong's family's rebuilding after a disaster create a claim? Is an unfinished sale between brothers a sale at all? Should a cousin's home give way to resort money? Is the village secretary's memory evidence, or pressure?

## Truth choices

- **Bong:** whether to admit he built beyond the old marker.
- **Marites:** what to certify, given her cousin's commission.

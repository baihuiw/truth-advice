# c05: The jade bangle

Cantonese household and a Filipina live-in helper, Sha Tin, Hong Kong (East Asia, with a Southeast Asian migrant) · care (domestic) · dementia · high stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A three-generation flat in a Sha Tin estate. Hong Kong households employ foreign domestic helpers who live in. A dismissed helper must leave Hong Kong within two weeks unless she finds a new employer, and a theft report can end her working life there. In Cantonese families, filial care is often carried out by a helper while the children work. A daughter who emigrated feels the guilt of distance. Jade bangles carry family meaning and are often promised to daughters.

## The event
Leung Wai-ying, a widow with moderate Alzheimer's, lives with her son Kenneth, his wife Winnie and their helper Joy, who has cared for her for nine years. In September 2026 Winnie searched Joy's drawer on her day off and found Mrs. Leung's green jade bangle wrapped in a handkerchief. Joy says Mrs. Leung gave it to her on her birthday in July, "for your girl's wedding." Kenneth's sister May, in Vancouver, says their mother promised her the bangle when she emigrated. Winnie wants to call the police; Kenneth has asked her to wait. Asked by Kenneth, Mrs. Leung said on Tuesday "I gave it to Joy" and on Thursday "someone took my bangle."

## People
- **Joy Dacanay** (41), live-in helper from Ilocos Norte; her daughter is engaged. *Only she knows:* the gift came in the morning, Mrs. Leung's clearest time, with "for your girl's wedding — don't tell Winnie." Joy tried twice to give it back, and Mrs. Leung became agitated both times.
- **Winnie Leung** (55), daughter-in-law, an office administrator. *Only she knows:* this year she quietly retrieved other things her mother-in-law gave away, a watch to the building guard and cash to a neighbour's grandchild.
- **Kenneth Leung** (58), son, a civil engineer. *Only he knows:* in April his mother told him "Joy is more a daughter to me than May." The bangle was bought by his father in 1979; it is not the grandmother's heirloom May believes it to be.
- **May Leung** (54), daughter, in Vancouver since 1997. *Only she knows:* on a video call last November, her mother called her "Joy".
- *Shared by Kenneth and Winnie:* they are pricing care homes and expect to let Joy go after the new year anyway. Dismissal for theft would also avoid notice and other payments.
- *Absent as a witness:* **Leung Wai-ying** (86), alive, with moderate Alzheimer's.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Mrs. Leung mean when she gave Joy the bangle?**
- Readings: `gift_meant` (a gift she understood and intended); `confused` (no real intention; the dementia); `safekeeping` (she wanted it kept safe from someone); `meant_may` (she meant it for her daughter, confusing Joy with May).
- Evidence: morning clarity and the specific reason, Joy's daughter's wedding → `gift_meant`; "don't tell Winnie" → `gift_meant` or `safekeeping`; the earlier giveaways (S6) → `confused`; calling May "Joy" (S5) and the 1997 promise (S2) → `meant_may`; "more a daughter than May" (S7) → `gift_meant`.
- Never settled: her accounts change from day to day, she cannot be questioned reliably, and no one else was present.

## Interpretive conflict
Is this theft, a gift, or a misunderstanding no one is to blame for? Does nine years of care give Joy a moral claim the family lacks? Does a daughter's absence weaken a decades-old promise? Is Winnie protecting the family, or using the bangle to end a contract cheaply?

## Truth choices
- **Joy:** whether to keep saying it was a gift, or to hand it back and say she "was only keeping it safe", a denial that might save her job and visa.
- **Winnie:** whether to tell Kenneth about the earlier giveaways, and whether to involve the police.
- **Kenneth:** whether to tell May what their mother said in April, and that the bangle is not an ancestral heirloom.
- **May:** whether to admit that her mother no longer knows her apart from Joy.

## Neutral friend
**Ho Siu-lan** (84), Mrs. Leung's friend of 30 years from Sunday dim sum and morning tai chi. She sees Joy at the park every morning and is Kenneth's godmother. May calls her for news. She knows the public facts and the confrontation.

## Values in tension
- Gratitude and affection against family property
- A migrant worker's vulnerability against an employer's suspicion
- Filial duty done by others against the guilt and claims of absent children
- Keeping one's job against standing by the truth

# c76: The flat on the ninth floor

## Setting and culture
In China a flat where someone has died an unnatural death is a *xiongzhai*, an "inauspicious house". Many people will not live in one, and its value and rent fall sharply. Courts have held that landlords and sellers must disclose such deaths, but what counts, and what a landlord "knew", is argued case by case. Elderly residents of old Shanghai lane-estate blocks often live alone, and a death may go unnoticed for days. For a young couple expecting a child, the in-laws' view of a flat's luck can matter as much as the rent.

## The event
In 2024 Mr. Gu, 76, a widower renting a ninth-floor flat in an old block in Shanghai, died alone in the flat and was found four days later. His son, who lives in Canada, cleared the flat and has not been back. The landlord, Mr. Qian, had the bathroom completely redone before advertising the flat again. In March 2026 Shen Jiayi and her husband signed a two-year lease through a young agent, Xiao Zhou, who said nothing about the death. Shen is now five months pregnant, and a neighbour has told her that "the old man died in there". The police record of the death is not released to third parties. Shen has asked Mr. Qian and Xiao Zhou directly what happened, and her mother-in-law visits next week.

## People
- **Shen Jiayi** (29), accountant, from Anhui. *Only she knows:* she and her husband cannot afford to break the lease and pay a new deposit, and she has not told him what the neighbour said.
- **Mr. Qian** (58), landlord; a commercial tie only. *Only he knows:* the property office told him in 2024 that Mr. Gu was found "on the bathroom floor", and he paid extra to have the whole bathroom replaced.
- **Xiao Zhou** (27), letting agent; no personal tie. *Only he knows:* the agency's internal notes on the flat say "elderly tenant deceased 2024, landlord says natural". His manager told him not to raise it unless asked.
- *Absent:* **Mr. Gu** (died aged 76); **his son**, in Canada.

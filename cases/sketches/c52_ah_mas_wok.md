# c52: Ah Ma's wok

Hokkien Chinese Malaysian, a hawker centre in George Town, Penang, Malaysia (Southeast Asia) · work (a family hawker business and its long-serving cook) · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend · estate-related (the stall and its name)

> **Status:** revise. Make the licence application to the council (registering the worker as family) the central choice.

## Setting and culture
Penang's hawker food is a point of civic pride, and famous stalls pass down through families with their recipes. Hawker licences are personal, and the city council allows a transfer mainly to family. Long-serving helpers who know "the fire" are often the real keepers of the taste. Restaurant chains now buy famous stall names for malls. Respect for elders and for their word runs deep, and talk among neighbouring stalls is constant.

## The event
Lim Siew Lan, "Ah Ma", ran a famous char koay teow stall from 1972. For 25 years Ah Seng, who is not family, fried beside her. In 2026 her granddaughter Jia Hui quit her bank job to learn the stall. Ah Ma died in July 2026. Ah Seng says that last year Ah Ma told him, "When I'm gone, the wok is yours." Jia Hui says Ah Ma told her, "Only you can keep the taste; take the stall." Ah Ma's son, Kok Leong, has an offer of 400,000 ringgit from a mall chain for the stall's name, "Ah Ma's", and the recipe. The licence must be renewed by January.

## People
- **Lim Jia Hui** (31), the granddaughter. *Only she knows:* she recorded Ah Ma on her phone teaching the recipe. At the end of the recording Ah Ma says, "Ah Seng knows the fire better than anyone. Listen to him."
- **Tan Ah Seng** (58), the long-serving cook. *Only he knows:* "the wok is yours" was said in 2025, just after he lent Ah Ma 30,000 ringgit for her hospital bills, which she never repaid. He also knows the lard and the dried-shrimp supplier that make the taste.
- **Lim Kok Leong** (60), Ah Ma's son, a property agent. *Only he knows:* in 2024 Ah Ma turned down the same chain, saying, "Never while I'm alive."
- *Absent:* **Lim Siew Lan, "Ah Ma"** (died aged 86).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Ah Ma mean by "the wok is yours"?**
- Readings: `the_stall` (the business itself); `the_pan` (the actual wok, as a keepsake or as repayment of the loan); `partnership` (that he would run it beside the family).
- Evidence: the timing just after the loan (S4) → `the_pan` as repayment, or `the_stall` as repayment; "Listen to him" (S6) → `partnership`; the family-only licence rule → `the_pan`.
- Never settled: she died, and nothing was written down.

**U2. Would Ah Ma have accepted selling the name after her death?**
- Readings: `never`; `after_me` (she meant only not in her lifetime); `if_family_agrees`.
- Evidence: "Never while I'm alive" (S3) → `after_me` or `never`; her pride in the stall → `never`.
- Never settled: she is dead.

## Interpretive conflict
Blood or service: who is the stall's heir? Is a recipe property, or heritage? Does a promise made after a loan count as a gift, a repayment, or a debt still owed? Is selling the name a betrayal of Ah Ma or a fair use of her success?

## Truth choices
- **Jia Hui:** whether to share the recording, which supports Ah Seng's role and weakens her own sole claim.
- **Ah Seng:** whether to reveal the loan. It reframes "the wok is yours" and gives him a separate claim to the money.
- **Kok Leong:** whether to tell the others his mother said "never while I'm alive."

## Neutral friend

# c39: Declared healed

Bemba-speaking Zambian (Pentecostal), a compound in Lusaka, Zambia (Southern Africa) · community (church) · death · high stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A crowded Lusaka compound where Pentecostal churches grow fast, and healing crusades promise to cure HIV, diabetes and blood pressure. Clinics run short of insulin, and buying it privately costs more than many traders earn in a week. Most pastors tell people to "confirm healing with the doctor," but members hear what they hear. Church choirs, funeral houses and loyalty to a charismatic pastor shape how a death is talked about.

## The event
Esther Mwansa, a market trader with type 2 diabetes on insulin, attended a healing crusade in July 2026. Pastor Joseph Banda laid hands on her and declared, "The sugar is gone, in Jesus's name." In August she stopped her insulin. On 3 September she collapsed with diabetic ketoacidosis and died in hospital. Her daughter Mutale blames the pastor and has told a radio programme. The church says it always tells members to keep taking their medicine. Mutale and the church are both preparing statements for the church council, and the radio station wants an interview with the pastor.

## People
- **Mutale Mwansa** (32), daughter, a bank teller. *Only she knows:* in June her mother said the insulin was too expensive and that she was "stretching" her doses. Mutale promised to help pay, and did not.
- **Pastor Joseph Banda** (48), founder of the church. *Only he knows:* at a private prayer session on 26 July, Esther asked him, "Should I stop the injections?" He answered, "Do what your faith tells you, mama," and not "keep taking them."
- **Grace Phiri** (45), deaconess. *Only she knows:* on 2 August she saw Esther throw her insulin pens into the church bin. Esther made her promise silence: "Don't tell the pastor; I want to surprise him with my testimony."
- **Chanda Mwansa** (60), Esther's husband, a minibus driver. *Only he knows:* he knew she had stopped, begged her to restart, and was refused. He did not tell Mutale, to avoid a fight, and he had also been short of money for the insulin.
- *Absent:* **Esther Mwansa** (died aged 57).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Why did Esther stop her insulin?**
- Readings: `believed_healed` (she believed she was cured); `cost` (she could not afford it, and faith gave a reason); `pastors_answer` (the pastor's reply decided it); `testimony` (to show her healing to the church).
- Evidence: the crusade (S2) → `believed_healed`; stretching doses over cost (S1) → `cost`; the private answer (S3) → `pastors_answer`; the pens in the bin and the planned surprise (S4) → `testimony`.
- Never settled: she died without explaining, and left no note.

## Interpretive conflict
Faith against medicine. Is a pastor responsible for what a member hears? Is the real killer the price of insulin? Did each person's silence, the husband's, the deaconess's and the daughter's broken promise, play its part? Should the church's name be protected?

## Truth choices
- **Pastor Banda:** whether to admit his answer on 26 July, to the council or on the radio.
- **Grace:** whether to tell Mutale about the pens and the planned testimony, breaking her promise.
- **Mutale:** whether to admit she failed to help with the cost.
- **Chanda:** whether to tell his daughter that he knew.

## Neutral friend
**Ruth Tembo** (63), a retired nurse and neighbour who belongs to a different church. She used to help Esther with her injections and knows Pastor Banda from community meetings. She knows the public facts and the radio programme.

## Values in tension
- Faith against medicine
- Loyalty to the church against truth
- Poverty against personal responsibility
- A promise of secrecy against a family's right to know

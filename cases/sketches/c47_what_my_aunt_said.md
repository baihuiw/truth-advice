# c47: What my aunt said at the funeral

French (Catholic bourgeoisie), Lyon, France (Europe) · family · death (with a lost record: a letter burned unread past its first line) · ordinary stakes · 2 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** keep.

## Setting and culture
In 1990s France, sperm donation through hospital centres was anonymous, and parents were advised to keep it secret. Since September 2022, people conceived with donated gametes can ask a national commission for their donor's identity, but donors from before the change must consent. In this kind of family, discretion is a virtue, and some things are not discussed even among siblings.

## The event
Philippe Lascombes, a notary, died of cancer in August 2026. At the funeral lunch, his sister Nathalie, who had been drinking, told his son Théo: "Your father wanted you to know. You're a donor child. He couldn't have children." Théo's mother, Sylvie, denied it that day and admitted it the next. She says Philippe never wanted Théo told, because Théo "was his son in every way." Théo has applied to the commission to contact his donor, and asks his mother again and again what his father really wanted.

## People
- **Théo Lascombes** (30), sound engineer. *Only he knows:* in 2023 he took a consumer DNA test out of curiosity. It showed no matches with his father's cousins, who had also tested. He suspected and said nothing. He remembers his father once saying, at a wedding, that "blood is overrated."
- **Sylvie Lascombes** (62), Théo's mother, a retired pharmacist. *Only she knows:* after his diagnosis in 2024, Philippe gave her a letter "to be opened by Théo after my death." After he died she read its first line, "*Mon fils, il y a une chose que je t'ai cachée…*" ("My son, there is something I have hidden from you…"), and burned it.
- *Absent:* **Philippe Lascombes** (died aged 66). **Nathalie** (63), his sister, is a game-master character.

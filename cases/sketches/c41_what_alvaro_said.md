# c41: What Álvaro said about it

Andalusian Spanish (Catholic), Seville, Spain (Europe) · care and medical (an organ donation decided in intensive care) · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
Spain leads the world in organ donation. In law, everyone is a presumed donor, but in practice transplant coordinators always ask the family, and a family's refusal is respected. Few people record their wishes. Many families decide on the strength of remarks the dead person once made. The Church supports donation. Recipients may send anonymous letters of thanks through the coordination office, and families may reply anonymously.

## The event
Álvaro Moreno crashed his motorbike in March 2026 and was declared brain dead the next day. The transplant coordinator spoke with his parents. His mother, Pilar, said at once that he had told her, "If anything happens, give it all." His father, Manuel, hesitated, then agreed. His heart, liver, kidneys and corneas were donated. In September his girlfriend, Lucía, told Manuel that a month before the crash, after a documentary, Álvaro had said, "I wouldn't want them cutting me up. I want to be buried whole." Manuel has hardly slept since. An anonymous letter of thanks from the heart recipient has now arrived, and the family must decide whether to answer.

## People
- **Pilar Moreno** (54), Álvaro's mother, a primary-school teacher. *Only she knows:* "give it all" was said in 2021, when Álvaro was eighteen, after his grandfather's kidney transplant. In 2023 he also joked, "Imagine they take everything and you wake up," and she has put that out of her mind.
- **Manuel Moreno** (57), Álvaro's father, an electrician. *Only he knows:* at the hospital the coordinator told him, "If you have doubts, we respect that." He agreed only because Pilar was so sure, and he has not told her he regrets it.
- **Lucía Ortega** (22), Álvaro's girlfriend of two years, a nursing student. *Only she knows:* in 2024 Álvaro showed her the confirmation email for a donor card he had applied for online. She has not mentioned it, because it undercuts what she told Manuel, and because Pilar has never accepted her.
- *Absent:* **Álvaro Moreno** (died aged 23).

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. What did Álvaro want done with his body?**
- Readings: `donate` (he wanted to donate); `not_donate` (he did not); `unsettled` (he had said both and never resolved it).
- Evidence: "give it all" (S1) and the donor-card application (S3) → `donate`; "buried whole" (S4) → `not_donate`; the 2023 joke (S2) → `unsettled`.
- Never settled: there is no binding record of his wishes, his remarks pointed both ways, and he is dead.

## Interpretive conflict
Whose word counts: the mother's, or the girlfriend's? Does a family decide for a son, or report his wishes? Was the decision rushed by Pilar's certainty? Does Lucía, who is not family, have any standing? Should they write back to the stranger who carries Álvaro's heart?

## Truth choices
- **Lucía:** whether to tell the parents about the donor-card application. It would ease Manuel and weaken her own account.
- **Pilar:** whether to admit the 2023 joke.
- **Manuel:** whether to tell Pilar he regrets agreeing.
- **The family:** whether to reply to the recipient, and what to say.

## Neutral friend

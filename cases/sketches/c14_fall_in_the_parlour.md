# c14: The fall in the parlour

Yoruba (Anglican), Ibadan, Nigeria, with children in Manchester and Toronto (West Africa and diaspora) · care (paid live-in carer) · death · ordinary stakes · 3 people + neutral friend

> **Status:** revise. Choices are generic; make the reference letter for the carer the institutional choice.

## Setting and culture
A quiet Ibadan estate of retired civil servants. Since about 2021 a wave of emigration (*japa*) has taken many adult children to the UK, Canada and the US. Ageing parents are left with paid live-in carers, often young women from other states, managed through a family WhatsApp group and monthly transfers. Elders are honoured and expect to decide for themselves. Burials follow church rites within weeks, and autopsies are rare.

## The event
Mrs. Comfort Adeyemi, a retired headmistress and widow, lived alone after all three children emigrated. Since 2024 she was cared for by Blessing Okon, a live-in carer from Akwa Ibom. On 11 August 2026 Blessing went out for about two hours. She came back to find Mrs. Adeyemi on the parlour floor near the edge of the rug, conscious but unable to speak. Mrs. Adeyemi died in hospital the next day; the doctor said "probably a stroke", and there was no autopsy. In the family WhatsApp group, the children's grief has turned on Blessing for leaving her alone. Blessing says Mama sent her. The burial is done, and the children are deciding whether to pay Blessing her last month and give her a reference.

## People
- **Blessing Okon** (31), the live-in carer, a widow with a son of ten at school in Ibadan. *Only she knows:* she was at her son's school prize-giving, and Mama insisted, "Go, I'm not a baby." That morning Mama had been dizzy and had refused the walking frame, as she had for weeks. She had forbidden Blessing to "worry the children".
- **Funmi Adeyemi-Clarke** (45), eldest daughter, a care-home team leader in Manchester. *Only she knows:* two days before, her mother said on the phone that she felt "light-headed". Funmi promised to arrange a doctor's visit and forgot, after a double shift.
- **Tunde Adeyemi** (42), son, an IT contractor in Toronto. *Only he knows:* in June he cut his share of Blessing's salary, without telling his sisters. Blessing had complained to him that she needed time off for her son. In July his mother asked him to "come home before it's late", and he said Christmas.
- *Absent:* **Comfort Adeyemi** (died aged 79). The youngest sibling, in Houston, is a game-master character.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Why did Comfort fall?**
- Readings: `stroke_first` (a stroke caused the fall, and nothing could have prevented it); `tripped` (an accident that a carer present, or the frame, might have prevented); `got_up_alone` (she got up despite feeling dizzy, a risk she chose); `untreated_dizziness` (a treatable cause such as blood pressure, which a doctor's visit might have caught).
- Evidence: the doctor's "probably a stroke" (S9) → `stroke_first`; dizziness that morning and two days before (S6, S7) → `untreated_dizziness` or `stroke_first`; the frame refused, and found at the rug's edge (S5, S8) → `tripped` or `got_up_alone`.
- Never settled: no one saw her fall, she could not speak in hospital, and there was no autopsy.

## Interpretive conflict
Is a paid carer to blame for two hours at her own child's event? Did children who left have the standing to blame someone who stayed? Whose call was it to accept the risk: Mama's, the carer's, the family's? Did a quiet pay cut push Blessing to the edge?

## Truth choices
- **Blessing:** whether to tell the children about the dizziness that morning. It supports a stroke, but it also shows she went out knowing.
- **Funmi:** whether to admit the "light-headed" call and the forgotten doctor.
- **Tunde:** whether to tell his sisters about the pay cut and his mother's plea.
- **All:** what to put in Blessing's reference.

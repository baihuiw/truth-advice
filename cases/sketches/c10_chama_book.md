# c10: The chama book

Kenyan (mixed Luo and Kikuyu members), an estate in eastern Nairobi (East Africa, with a migrant in the UK) · community (women's savings group) · departure (with a lost record) · ordinary stakes · 4 people + neutral friend

## Setting and culture
A *chama*, a women's savings group of twelve market traders and hairdressers in a fictional estate, Mihango Gardens. It runs a merry-go-round payout and an investment fund saving toward a plot. Money moves by M-Pesa and through a SACCO account; trust rests on a handwritten ledger book and on the treasurer's name. Since 2023 many Kenyan women have left for care work in the UK. Members also pray together at a Sunday fellowship; shame and reputation matter a great deal.

## The event
The group had saved 1.2 million shillings toward land when its treasurer of five years, Mercy Atieno, left for a care-worker job in Leicester in March 2026. She sent one voice note: "I've left the book with Mama Wanjiru." By May she had blocked the group. The SACCO statement shows 340,000 shillings withdrawn in cash, in five withdrawals between November and February. The chairlady says she received a bag of receipts but no ledger. Mercy's sister Njeri says the book was at their mother's house in Machakos, which flooded in April. A special meeting on 1 November will decide whether to report Mercy to the police and to her UK employer.

## People
- **Wanjiru Kamau** (52), the chairlady, a market trader. *Only she knows:* in May 2025 she borrowed 80,000 from the fund informally through Mercy, for her son's school fees, and never repaid it. In the bag of receipts she found a page in Mercy's handwriting headed "loans: W 80, ..." and removed it.
- **Akinyi Odhiambo** (38), member and Mercy's close friend, a hairdresser. *Only she knows:* in September Mercy sent her 20,000 shillings with the message "for the group, quietly, more later." Akinyi has not handed it over.
- **Njeri Wambui** (35), Mercy's half-sister and a member. *Only she knows:* she saw the ledger's pages pulped and unreadable after the flood.
- **Otieno Ouma** (47), Mercy's husband, a matatu driver who stayed in Nairobi. *Only he knows:* in January Mercy paid off his 150,000 debt to a moneylender. He never asked where the money came from.
- *Absent:* **Mercy Atieno** (44), in Leicester, not responding.

## Permanently unknowable
**U1. Where did the missing 340,000 go?**
- Readings: `mercy_took` (she kept it); `unrecorded_loans` (it was lent to members off the books); `husband_debt` (it cleared Otieno's debt, and she meant to repay); `mixed` (part loans, part debt).
- Evidence: Wanjiru's own loan (S2) and the removed page (S6) → `unrecorded_loans`; the January debt payment (S4) → `husband_debt`; the 20,000 "for the group" (S9) → repayment intent; her silence and blocking (S8) → `mercy_took`.
- Never settled: the ledger is destroyed, the statement shows only cash withdrawals, and Mercy will not speak to them. She is unlikely ever to return.

**U2. What did Mercy mean by the 20,000?**
- Readings: `first_repayment`; `buy_silence` (to keep Akinyi on her side); `personal` (a debt or gift between friends).
- Evidence: "for the group" → `first_repayment`; "quietly" → `buy_silence`; sent to a friend, not to the chair → `personal` or `buy_silence`.
- Never settled: she will not explain.

## Interpretive conflict
Theft, muddle, or a desperate wife's borrowing? Can the chairlady judge a treasurer while hiding her own loan? Do loose rules make the whole group responsible? Is reporting a woman to her foreign employer justice or ruin?

## Truth choices
- **Wanjiru:** whether to confess her loan and the removed page before the meeting.
- **Akinyi:** whether to hand over the 20,000 and show the message, which proves Mercy is in contact.
- **Njeri:** whether to tell the group the book was destroyed, knowing it may sound like a cover story.
- **Otieno:** whether to admit his debt was paid in January.

## Neutral friend
**Grace Mutua** (63), a founding member who left the *chama* in 2024 when she moved in with her daughter. She still leads the Sunday fellowship prayers that most members attend, and everyone confides in her. She knows the public facts and the statement.

## Values in tension
- Collective trust against loyalty to a friend or sister
- Accountability against mercy for a migrant woman
- Authority against hypocrisy
- Shame against disclosure

## Source and fictionalisation
- **Source type:** Kenyan news reports on *chama* treasurers vanishing with savings, used for structure only: https://biznakenya.com/mombasa-chama/
- **Taken:** a treasurer who leaves with savings unaccounted for, and members reconstructing the books.
- **Invented:** emigration as the departure, the destroyed ledger, the chair's hidden loan, the 20,000, and all people and places.
